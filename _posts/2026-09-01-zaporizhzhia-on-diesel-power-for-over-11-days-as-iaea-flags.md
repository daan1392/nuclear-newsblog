The Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant had been running on emergency diesel generators for more than 11 days, and the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) said it was worried about fuel supplies if the outage continued.

The plant has been under Russian military control since early March 2022. It lost its last operating external power line on 20 August. Since then the generators have supplied the electricity needed for the pumps that cool its six reactors and its used fuel ponds.

The IAEA team at the site was told that a diesel delivery planned for May had been postponed because of military activity. On Thursday and Friday, IAEA experts observed the transfer of several dozen tonnes of diesel fuel to the site. The agency still raised concerns about how long reserves would last without a restored grid connection.

Emergency diesel generators are a last line of defence for cooling. Even in shutdown reactors, decay heat from the fuel must be removed continuously, so a long dependence on backup power leaves little margin if fuel deliveries are disrupted.
