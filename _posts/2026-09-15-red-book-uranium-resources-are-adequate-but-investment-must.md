The latest *Uranium 2026: Resources, Production and Demand* report concluded that the world has enough uranium to support nuclear growth, provided investment in mining keeps increasing.

The publication, known as the Red Book, is produced jointly by the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency and the IAEA. It is the 31st edition in a series stretching back more than 60 years and relies mostly on data supplied by governments. It covers 2023 and 2024, with some information from 2025 and 2026, and was launched at the 70th IAEA General Conference in Vienna.

As of 1 January 2025, identified resources recoverable at costs below USD260 per kilogram of uranium stood at more than 8.1 million tonnes of uranium, up 2.1%. Spending on exploration and mine development has risen substantially in recent years, the report noted, reflecting the sector's response to renewed interest in expanding nuclear capacity.

The authors stressed, however, that resources in the ground are not the same as supply. Turning them into production requires further, sustained investment if uranium output is to match expected growth in demand for nuclear energy.
