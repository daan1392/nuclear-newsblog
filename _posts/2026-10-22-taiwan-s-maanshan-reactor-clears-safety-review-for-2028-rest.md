Taiwan's nuclear safety authority completed its technical review of the restart plan for Maanshan Nuclear Power Plant, clearing the way for operational restart potentially as early as 2028. The plant houses two 900 MWe pressurized water reactors.

This development follows Taiwan's May 2025 shutdown of its last operating reactor and a subsequent legislative change allowing reactors to operate for up to 60 years total—a 20-year extension beyond previous limits. Taiwan's state-owned utility Taipower submitted the Maanshan restart application in March 2026, supported by the government's economic affairs ministry.

The regulatory approval represents a major policy reversal. Taiwan had phased out nuclear power but faces mounting electricity demand and climate pressures. Restarting closed reactors provides a relatively rapid pathway to increase clean generation capacity compared to building new facilities. The Maanshan restart, once operational, could provide significant carbon-free electricity to the grid.
