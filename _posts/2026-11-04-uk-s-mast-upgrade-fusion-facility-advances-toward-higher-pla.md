The UK Atomic Energy Authority installed a 12-tonne double beam box at the MAST Upgrade tokamak at Culham, a facility designed to double the machine's neutral beam injection heating power to 8.8 megawatts. The new component features two additional neutral beam injectors capable of raising plasma temperatures from the current 30 million degrees Celsius to 60 million degrees.

Neutral beam injection—the process of firing high-energy neutral atoms into the plasma to increase its temperature—is a critical plasma heating and control technique used in most tokamaks. The upgrade enables MAST Upgrade to investigate physics and technologies essential for future fusion power plants, including plasma behaviour at higher temperatures and improved confinement conditions.

MAST Upgrade is the UK's flagship fusion research machine and supports research into the tokamak approach to magnetic confinement fusion. The facility continues to contribute to international fusion knowledge while advancing the technical readiness of fusion reactor concepts.
