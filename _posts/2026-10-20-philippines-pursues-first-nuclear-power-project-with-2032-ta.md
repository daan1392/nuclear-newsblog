The Philippines announced plans to establish the policy, regulatory and commercial foundations for its first nuclear power project, with an initial target of 1.2 GW of capacity by 2032 and longer-term ambitions to reach 4.8 GW by 2050. The announcement came at the World Nuclear Supply Chain conference in Manila.

The Philippines faces rising electricity demand driven by economic growth and industrial expansion. The government is evaluating nuclear power as part of its energy diversification strategy, alongside renewable sources and other low-carbon options. Development of the regulatory framework and vendor qualification processes represents the current phase, preceding site selection and project implementation.

The Philippines had operated a single nuclear unit at Bataan from 1984 until its shutdown in 1986 following the Chernobyl accident, leaving a 40-year gap in domestic nuclear experience. Reintroduction of nuclear power requires rebuilding regulatory capacity, workforce training and public confidence. The announced timeline reflects gradual institutional development needed to support safe, secure nuclear operations and align with international safety and nonproliferation standards.
