Radiation from space reaching the altitudes used by commercial aircraft increases as solar activity declines, according to a new study based on high-altitude balloon measurements.

Radiation from space constantly enters Earth's atmosphere. How much of it reaches aviation altitudes depends on height and location, and also on the sun's activity. The researchers flew balloons over southern Israel and mapped how radiation changed through the atmosphere. They found that levels at flight altitudes rose when the sun was less active.

The result is relevant to radiation protection for aircrew and frequent flyers, since cosmic radiation is a natural source of exposure at cruising altitude. Measurements of this kind help refine dose estimates across the solar cycle. The summary available did not give dose figures or describe the study's dates, so the practical size of the effect is not stated here.
