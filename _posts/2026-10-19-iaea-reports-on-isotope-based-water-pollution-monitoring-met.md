The International Atomic Energy Agency completed a four-year coordinated research project involving 14 countries to develop and validate isotope-based methods for tracing nitrate pollution in river and groundwater systems. The participating nations included Argentina, Australia, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ecuador, Finland, France, Greece, Italy, Malaysia, Nigeria, Pakistan, Spain and Ukraine.

Isotope tracing leverages the distinct isotopic signatures of nitrogen in different pollution sources—fertilizers, sewage, atmospheric deposition—to identify contamination origins and track pollutant movement through aquifers and watersheds. These techniques offer advantages over conventional chemical analysis by providing source discrimination and mobility data without complex and costly monitoring infrastructure.

The published report documents validated protocols and demonstrates practical applications in diverse hydrogeological settings across multiple climate zones and development contexts. Results support regulatory agencies and water authorities in targeting pollution control efforts and validating remediation strategies. The IAEA initiative exemplifies how nuclear science and isotope techniques contribute to environmental protection and sustainable water resource management.
