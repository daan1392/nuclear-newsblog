Romania's two Cernavodă reactor units were reconnected to the grid following a three-month shutdown forced by drought-related low water levels in the Danube River. The first unit was reconnected on November 6, with the second beginning start-up procedures and synchronising to the grid on November 8.

Both units were shut down as a precaution when summer drought in central Europe caused the Danube's water level to drop below the threshold required for adequate cooling water supply. Nuclearelectrica and the Romanian government are now working on long-term solutions to prevent future shutdowns from similar climate events. The incident illustrates growing operational challenges for reactor sites dependent on river cooling as drought and climate variability increase.
