The Wendelstein 7-X (W7-X) stellarator at the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics in Greifswald, Germany, began its OP2.4 experimental campaign in mid-September, continuing through mid-December. More than 100 scientists from approximately 20 European laboratories are collaborating to push the device beyond previous results and uncover new fusion physics.

Unlike a tokamak, which uses strong electrical currents to confine plasma, the stellarator confines fusion fuel using a complex three-dimensional twisted magnetic field geometry. This approach offers inherent plasma stability without current-driven disruptions, though the physics is more complex. Each experimental campaign is critical for advancing understanding of stellarator physics and improving pathway to fusion energy.

W7-X is a leading flagship facility for the stellarator fusion concept in Europe. Results from OP2.4 are expected to inform the design of future demonstration and commercial stellarator reactors, supporting Europe's long-term fusion energy roadmap.
