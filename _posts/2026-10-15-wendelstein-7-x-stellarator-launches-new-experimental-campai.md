The Wendelstein 7-X (W7-X) stellarator in Greifswald, Germany began its OP2.4 experimental campaign in mid-September, running through mid-December and hosting more than 100 scientists from approximately 20 European laboratories. The campaign aims to push the device beyond its previous operational results and explore new physics phenomena.

Unlike tokamaks, which rely on a strong toroidal electric current to confine plasma, stellarators use a twisted, fully three-dimensional magnetic field geometry. This configuration provides natural plasma stability without current-driven instabilities, but introduces greater physics complexity. Each experimental campaign at W7-X generates data to refine understanding of stellarator confinement and performance, informing future reactor designs.

W7-X remains the world's most advanced stellarator and represents a key pathway toward alternative magnetic confinement fusion. Results from OP2.4 will inform ongoing international discussions about reactor-scale stellarator concepts and whether the design path offers practical advantages over tokamak approaches for future power generation.
