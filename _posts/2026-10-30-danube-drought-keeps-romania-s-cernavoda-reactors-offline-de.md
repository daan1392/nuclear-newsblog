Nuclearelectrica maintained both Cernavoda units offline following the unprecedented summer drought that reduced Danube water levels below operational thresholds. Unit 1 shut down on 28 July and unit 2 on 12 August when water levels fell below the 185-centimetre mark required for normal plant operations; levels had reached a record low of 182 centimetres. Although a recent rise in local water levels provided some improvement, current meteorological forecasts did not support restart decisions as of late October. The extended shutdown underscores climate-related challenges to nuclear plant operations in water-stressed regions and reflects the sector's vulnerability to hydrological extremes exacerbated by drought conditions affecting central European waterways.
