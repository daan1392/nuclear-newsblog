The US Department of Commerce has outlined a USD 120 billion framework agreement between the United States and South Korea to construct eight new nuclear reactors—six Westinghouse AP1000 units and two South Korean APR1400 units. The framework allocates USD 100 billion for overnight construction costs and USD 20 billion in contingency reserves. Specific sites have not yet been designated. The initiative, branded 'Project Power', is aligned with the US administration's goal of having ten large reactors under construction by 2030, part of a broader strategy to expand domestic nuclear capacity and decarbonize the electricity system. The agreement pools US technological expertise with South Korean nuclear engineering capability, leveraging proven designs to accelerate deployment. The AP1000, a Westinghouse pressurized water reactor, has completed construction globally and demonstrated operational performance. The APR1400, South Korea's advanced commercial reactor, brings additional design maturity and export experience to the partnership.
