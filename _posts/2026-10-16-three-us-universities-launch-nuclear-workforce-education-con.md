Three major US universities launched the New Nuclear Academic Consortium to address documented gaps in the nuclear workforce through development of microcredentials and immersive training tools. Arizona State University, Purdue University, and the University of Tennessee–Knoxville partnered to broaden nuclear education access beyond traditional degree pathways.

The consortium responds to surging demand for nuclear professionals driven by reactor life extensions, new construction projects, and expanded applications in energy, medicine, and research. Microcredentials provide targeted competency certification in specialized areas, allowing working professionals and career changers to acquire skills relevant to specific roles.

The initiative supports the nuclear industry's human capital needs as retirement rates accelerate and technology complexity increases. By creating accessible pathways to nuclear competency, the consortium aims to diversify the talent pipeline and prepare the workforce for both conventional and advanced reactor technologies.
