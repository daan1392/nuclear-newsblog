Google has signed a long-term power purchase agreement (PPA) with Finnish utility Fortum for up to 50% of the capacity of the Loviisa nuclear power plant.

The contract covers the plant's life-extension period. It starts in 2028 at reduced capacity and then covers 50% of capacity from 2030 to 2049. It gives Fortum financial certainty for power uprates, meaning increases in output, and for lifetime extensions to 2050. Loviisa has two VVER-440 pressurised water reactors and was Finland's first nuclear plant.

The agreement came as Google said it would invest EUR13 billion (USD15 billion) in data centres and supporting infrastructure in Finland over 2027–2028, with projects in Hamina, Muhos, Vaala and Kajaani.

At the World Nuclear Symposium Finance Summit in London on the same day, Google's Lucia Tian, Director of Advanced Energy Technologies, said the company had received inbound approaches from more than a hundred potential providers in the nuclear space in the past year. The Loviisa deal shows large electricity buyers turning to existing plants as well as new designs, and that financiers increasingly see nuclear projects as investable.
