A real-world analysis from a French early-access program published in The Journal of Nuclear Medicine confirmed that prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted radiopharmaceutical therapy delivers clinical benefit for men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) consistent with findings from the pivotal VISION trial. The study represents the largest real-world cohort of PSMA-therapy patients analyzed to date and validates the therapy's benefit-risk profile in routine clinical practice.

PSMA radiopharmaceutical therapy involves targeting cancer cells expressing PSMA, a surface antigen highly prevalent in prostate tumors. The therapy combines diagnostic and therapeutic functions—"theranostics"—using radiolabeled compounds to image disease and deliver tumoricidal radiation directly to tumor sites. The French program data provide evidence on patient selection criteria and usage trends, informing clinical protocols beyond trial settings.

The analysis supports broader adoption of PSMA-directed therapies as standard of care for mCRPC, particularly in healthcare systems where regulatory approval has lagged initial trial publication. Real-world validation reduces clinical uncertainty, addresses economic decision-making by payers, and accelerates treatment access for patients with limited remaining options. Growing body evidence for PSMA and parallel advances in other radiopharmaceutical targets position nuclear medicine as an increasingly important component of precision oncology.
