The US nuclear sector is facing acute skills shortages as utilities move forward with fleet expansion, prompting coordinated efforts to build workforce capacity. Westinghouse and the University of South Carolina announced a partnership to create a multidisciplinary nuclear workforce development programme tailored to support deployment of AP1000 pressurized water reactors across the US fleet.

Separately, the New York Power Authority awarded USD10 million in funding to nuclear workforce development programmes operating throughout the state. The dual initiatives reflect recognition across industry and government that skilled labor—from engineers to construction trades to operations staff—is essential for near-term reactor construction and long-term plant operation.

These moves align with broader US policy priorities under the Inflation Reduction Act and bipartisan support for domestic nuclear expansion. Universities, utilities and vendors are collaborating to create pipelines from high school through advanced degrees, with emphasis on hands-on training at operating plants and construction sites. The efforts address both immediate hiring needs and longer-term succession planning as experienced nuclear professionals approach retirement.
