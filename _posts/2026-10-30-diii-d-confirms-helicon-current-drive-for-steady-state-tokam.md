The DIII-D National Fusion Facility has published experimental results in Physical Review Letters confirming helicon current drive, a technique that could enable steady-state operation of tokamak-based fusion power plants. Helicon current drive represents a new approach to sustaining the electric currents necessary for plasma confinement without relying entirely on neutral beam injection or other conventional methods.

Steady-state operation is essential for commercial fusion reactors, as pulsed operation limits energy output and efficiency. The successful demonstration of helicon current drive expands the toolkit for fusion researchers designing tokamaks that can operate continuously at the power levels and plasma conditions required for electricity generation.

The result advances the practical engineering of magnetic confinement fusion systems and contributes to ongoing efforts to translate fusion concepts from research facilities into demonstrator and commercial power plants.
