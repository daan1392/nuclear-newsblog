Nucleon Fuel, a subsidiary of Canadian small modular reactor developer Nucleon Energy, applied for a USD 20 million grant and possible USD 10 million loan from Wyoming's Energy Dominance Fund to construct a uranium conversion facility near Gillette. Both grants and loans would be funded by Wyoming's recently created Energy Dominance Fund, which supports projects in oil, natural gas, coal and nuclear energy.

Uranium conversion is a critical fuel cycle step that transforms uranium ore concentrates (yellowcake) into uranium hexafluoride gas suitable for enrichment or use in reactor fuel. Establishing domestic conversion capacity strengthens the US nuclear fuel supply chain and reduces dependence on foreign suppliers, particularly important given geopolitical tensions affecting energy security.

The project reflects state-level interest in developing integrated nuclear fuel cycle capabilities and the strategic importance of domestic uranium processing. Wyoming, a major uranium-producing state, has existing mining infrastructure and regulatory experience that could support fuel cycle development.
