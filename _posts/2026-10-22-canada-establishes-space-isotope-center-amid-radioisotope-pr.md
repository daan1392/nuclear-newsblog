The Canadian Nuclear Innovation Institute established the Canadian Centre for Space Isotopes as Canada advanced its space nuclear capabilities. Concurrently, the Canadian Space Agency announced C$11.1 million in funding for 16 space projects, with two specifically focused on radioisotope technologies for aerospace applications.

Radioisotopes and radioisotope power systems (RTGs) enable long-duration space missions where solar power is insufficient, particularly in deep space, planetary exploration, and missions requiring extended operational lifetimes. The new center will coordinate research, development, and commercialization of space isotope applications, addressing both near-term satellite and probe requirements and longer-term exploration missions.

Canada's investment reflects both scientific interest and industrial opportunity. Reliable radioisotope supply chains remain constrained globally, and domestic production and application expertise can support both Canadian space missions and international partnerships. The funding allocation suggests the Canadian government views space nuclear technology as strategically important for national space capabilities and positions Canadian industry for participation in international deep-space exploration programs expected to expand through the 2030s.
