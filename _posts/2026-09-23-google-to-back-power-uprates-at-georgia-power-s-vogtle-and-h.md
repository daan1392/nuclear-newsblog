Georgia Power and Google have agreed that the technology company will support extended power uprates at the utility's Vogtle and Hatch nuclear plants, adding roughly 96 MW of capacity.

An extended power uprate (EPU) raises a reactor's licensed thermal power through targeted upgrades to equipment such as turbines, pumps, motors and cooling systems. The extra heat increases steam flow to the turbine generator, so the plant produces more electricity from its existing reactor design and infrastructure.

According to the announcement and filings with the Georgia Public Service Commission (PSC) on Monday, the utility will request an EPU for the two units at Hatch, near Baxley. The PSC must still approve that request. An uprate for Vogtle units 1 and 2, near Waynesboro, was already approved by the PSC in 2025.

The deal is another example of large technology firms supporting additional nuclear output from existing plants. Uprates are attractive because they add capacity at sites that already have licences, grid connections and operating workforces.
