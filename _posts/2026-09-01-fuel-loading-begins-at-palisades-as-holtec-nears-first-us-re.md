Holtec International has begun loading fuel into the reactor at Palisades in Michigan, a major step toward the first restart of a US nuclear unit that had been closed for decommissioning.

The core will hold 204 fuel assemblies, including some partially used assemblies left from the plant's final operating cycles before it closed in 2022. Palisades is a single-unit, 805 MWe pressurised water reactor that began commercial operation in 1971. Its previous owner, Entergy, announced in 2016 that it planned to close the plant.

Holtec's Chief Nuclear Officer Fadi Diya called the fuel load "an important milestone". He said the company remained focused on carrying out the remaining work and returning the plant to safe and reliable operation.

Restarting a reactor that has been formally shut down is unusual. Palisades is being watched as a possible model for other closed plants, though the remaining work still has to be completed before the unit can return to service.
