Poland's Ministry of Energy, Polskie Elektrownie Jądrowe (the national nuclear operator), and Polska Grupa Energetyczna (the state energy group) signed an agreement establishing the division of tasks and cooperation principles for preparing construction of Poland's second nuclear power plant. The agreement formalizes institutional arrangements for moving the project from planning toward implementation.

Poland's nuclear strategy, adopted in September 2021, envisions six large pressurized water reactors with combined capacity of 6–9 GWe to be built by 2040, significantly reducing the country's dependence on coal for electricity generation. The first plant is scheduled to begin construction in 2026, with the initial 1.0–1.6 GWe unit expected to be commissioned in the early 2030s. The new agreement addresses governance structures and responsibilities for the second plant, ensuring coordinated technical and financial planning.

The formalization of cooperation between key stakeholders demonstrates Poland's commitment to a structured, multi-unit nuclear expansion. Clear institutional frameworks are essential for managing the technical, financial, and regulatory complexity of large capital projects in the nuclear sector.
