Researchers from the University of Michigan College of Engineering and Switzerland's Paul Scherrer Institute have developed a new airborne radiation detection method that identifies single or mixed radiation sources with accuracy in determining identity, activity, and location. The research, published in *Nature Communications* as "Quantitative Mobile Gamma-Ray Spectrometry through Bayesian Inference," uses advanced statistical analysis of aerial gamma-ray spectroscopy data.

The technique addresses a critical capability gap for emergency responders facing radiological hazards such as reactor accidents or radiological dispersal events. Rapid, accurate determination of source location and intensity enables faster, more precise evacuation planning and protective action decisions, including distribution of protective equipment and potassium iodide.

The method improves on traditional airborne survey approaches by providing quantitative source identification rather than simple hotspot mapping, supporting informed decisions about shelter-in-place versus evacuation and targeted protective measures. Such capabilities have become increasingly important for nuclear security and emergency preparedness.
