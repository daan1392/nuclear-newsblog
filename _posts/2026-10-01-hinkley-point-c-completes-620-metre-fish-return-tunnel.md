A tunnel boring machine has completed excavation of a 620-metre tunnel as part of Hinkley Point C's fish protection system in the Bristol Channel. The five-month tunnelling project brings the total marine tunnels dug for the new nuclear power station in southwest England to more than eight kilometres.

Coastal power stations use seawater for cooling, and Hinkley Point C's design includes specially engineered intake heads that slow water entry into cooling tunnels, allowing fish to escape before reaching the intake system. The completed tunnel forms a critical part of this environmental mitigation approach.

The tunnel boring machine has been retrieved by floating crane and will be cleaned and moved to another project site. The achievement represents substantial progress on the UK's largest new nuclear construction project, which continues to advance through its building phases.
