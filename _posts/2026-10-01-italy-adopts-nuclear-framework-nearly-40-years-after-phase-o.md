Italy has adopted legislation establishing a regulatory framework for nuclear power generation, marking a historic reversal of the country's nuclear policy nearly 40 years after a referendum-driven phase-out. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni characterized the decision as "a courageous and sensible choice" during the legislative vote.

The framework now opens the door for Italy to develop nuclear projects after decades of relying on imported electricity from neighbouring countries' reactors. French and other European firms, including EDF and the SMR developer Nuward, have already signalled interest in participating in Italy's nuclear programme. The regulatory adoption is a first step; implementation will require translating the framework into specific rules and operational procedures.

Italy's move joins a broader European trend of countries revisiting or expanding nuclear capacity, driven by climate commitments, energy security concerns, and electricity demand growth. While concrete projects remain to be defined and approved, the legislative framework removes a fundamental barrier to nuclear development in one of Europe's largest economies.
