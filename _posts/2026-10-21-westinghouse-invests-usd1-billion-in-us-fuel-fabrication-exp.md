Westinghouse announced a USD1 billion investment in the Columbia Fuel Fabrication Facility in Hopkins, South Carolina, marking the first new fleet-scale nuclear fuel factory to be built in the USA in over 50 years. The facility aims to begin operations in 2030.

The expansion focuses on producing LEU+ fuel, which features higher uranium enrichment levels than the standard low-enriched uranium currently used in most commercial reactors. LEU+ was first deployed commercially in a US reactor in 2025, with lead assemblies containing accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) assemblies loaded into operations.

The investment addresses a critical supply chain vulnerability as nuclear deployment accelerates. Domestic fuel fabrication capacity has remained constrained since the 1970s, and plans to extend existing reactor lifespans and build new capacity require expanded production capabilities. Westinghouse's commitment signals growing confidence in sustained US nuclear demand.
