Taiwan's nuclear regulatory authority completed its examination of the restart plan for the Maanshan Nuclear Power Plant, removing a major regulatory hurdle for the nation's return to nuclear generation. The government projects that the facility's first unit could resume operations as early as 2028, following years of shutdown.

Maanshan comprises two 900 MWe pressurized water reactors. Taiwan halted its last operating reactor in May 2025, leaving the island without nuclear generation capacity. However, legislative changes in 2025 authorized reactor operation for up to 60 years total, and in March 2026 the state utility Taipower submitted formal restart documentation for Maanshan, catalyzing the recent regulatory review.

Taiwan's rekindling of nuclear generation reflects shifting energy policy driven by electricity demand growth, industrial decarbonization commitments, and concerns about relying heavily on imported fossil fuels and weather-dependent renewables. The regulatory approval provides momentum for Maanshan's restart and signals potential pathways for other idle Taiwanese units. Regional context matters: Taiwan's decision contrasts with policy in neighboring Japan and South Korea and may influence broader Asian sentiment toward nuclear as a climate mitigation technology.
