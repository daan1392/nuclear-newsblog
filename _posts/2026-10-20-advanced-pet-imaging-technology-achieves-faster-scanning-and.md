Researchers presented advances in positron emission tomography (PET) imaging technology at the 39th Annual Congress of the European Association of Nuclear Medicine in October. A next-generation total-body PET/CT scanner achieved diagnostic-quality images in as little as one minute of scanning, with image quality ratings exceeding those from standard PET/CT systems.

In a separate study, a new PET imaging approach detected 97.9 percent of lesions in clear cell renal cell carcinoma, the most common form of kidney cancer. The approach could provide clinicians with more complete information about disease location and extent, potentially improving treatment planning and patient outcomes.

These developments reflect ongoing efforts to reduce patient scanning times and radiation exposure while improving diagnostic accuracy. Faster imaging times increase clinical throughput and reduce patient discomfort, while higher detection sensitivity enables earlier identification of disease spread. The improvements demonstrate the continued evolution of nuclear medicine imaging as a cornerstone of oncology practice.
