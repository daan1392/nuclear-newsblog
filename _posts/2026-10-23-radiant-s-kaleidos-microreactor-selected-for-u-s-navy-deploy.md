The U.S. Navy announced on October 22 that it selected Radiant Industries to design, build, and operate a microreactor power plant at Naval Weapons Station Crane in Indiana, featuring the company's 1 MW Kaleidos design. Radiant is now the only company to have secured deployment agreements with all three U.S. military departments—Army, Air Force, and Navy—marking a significant endorsement of its transportable reactor technology.

The Kaleidos is a factory-built, high-temperature gas-cooled unit rated at 1 MW with a designed operational life of 20 years on a single fuel load lasting approximately five years. The system requires no cooling water, minimal excavation, and no extensive on-site construction, positioning it for rapid deployment at remote or austere locations. Radiant describes the reactor as "plug-in ready" for direct operation following site delivery.

The Navy deployment reflects broader U.S. military interest in distributed, resilient power sources for forward-operating bases and critical installations. Success with these prototype units could inform future force structure decisions and demonstrate the operational feasibility of microreactors in demanding environments. The convergence of Army, Air Force, and Navy selections suggests growing confidence in the technology's reliability and maintenance requirements within the military market.
