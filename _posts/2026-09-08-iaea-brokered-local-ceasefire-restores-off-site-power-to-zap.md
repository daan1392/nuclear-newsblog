The Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant in Ukraine was reconnected to external electricity after an outage of 18 days, following a local ceasefire arranged by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).

The plant's last remaining external line, Ferosplavna-1, was lost on 20 August after military activity north of the Dnipro River. Since then, the six-unit site had relied on its emergency diesel generators for the power needed to cool its reactors and spent fuel ponds. Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi said the plant faced a possible station blackout, meaning a total loss of AC power, as its diesel reserves dwindled.

The ceasefire, the seventh the IAEA has helped arrange at the site, covered demining and repair work along the line. Grossi told the agency's Board of Governors on Monday that both sides had engaged constructively, showing that practical steps to improve nuclear safety were possible "even in extremely difficult circumstances".

Grossi said restored off-site power allows the plant to again draw on external electricity for cooling and for other essential safety and security functions. The episode shows how dependent the site remains on a single fragile connection. Its reactors are not generating power, but they and the stored spent fuel still need continuous cooling.
