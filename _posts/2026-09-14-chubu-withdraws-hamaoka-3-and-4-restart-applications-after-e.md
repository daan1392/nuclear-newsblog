Chubu Electric Power said it would withdraw its applications for safety reviews of units 3 and 4 at the Hamaoka nuclear plant in Shizuoka Prefecture, after an independent committee concluded that workers felt pressure from senior management to manipulate earthquake data.

The company had applied to Japan's Nuclear Regulation Authority (NRA) in February 2014 for unit 4 and June 2015 for unit 3. Both boiling water reactors had been under review to check whether the plant's measures meet Japan's post-Fukushima safety standards.

On 5 January this year, Chubu disclosed "inappropriate matters" in how the design basis ground motion was formulated for the review. This figure is the earthquake shaking level that a plant must be designed to withstand, and it underpins much of the safety case. Chubu then set up an independent committee to investigate, and its findings led to the decision to withdraw.

The withdrawal ends, for now, the path to restarting the two units. It also underlines how central the integrity of submitted data is to Japan's licensing process.
