The International Atomic Energy Agency published a report on isotope-based methods for tracing nitrate pollution in river and groundwater systems, culminating a four-year coordinated research project involving 14 countries: Argentina, Australia, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ecuador, Finland, France, Greece, Italy, Malaysia, Nigeria, Pakistan, Spain and Ukraine.

Isotope tracing exploits distinct nitrogen and oxygen isotopic signatures of different pollution sources—atmospheric deposition, synthetic fertilizers, wastewater and animal waste. This fingerprinting enables scientists to discriminate pollutant origins and track migration pathways through aquifers, critical for protecting drinking water supplies and managing agricultural contamination.

The research demonstrates the practical utility of nuclear science techniques for environmental stewardship in water-stressed regions. Isotope methods complement conventional chemical testing without generating radioactive waste, providing cost-effective environmental monitoring particularly valuable for developing nations facing nutrient pollution challenges.
