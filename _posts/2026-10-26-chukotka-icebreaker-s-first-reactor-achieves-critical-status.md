The starboard reactor unit on Russia's Chukotka nuclear icebreaker reached minimum controlled power level, marking a major operational milestone for the vessel. The icebreaker is equipped with two RITM-200 reactors, each producing 175 MW of thermal power, as part of the Project 22220 fleet designed for Arctic operations.

Each reactor unit weighs over 147 tonnes and measures 7.3 metres tall with a 3.3-metre diameter. The reactors are engineered to withstand harsh Arctic conditions and harsh ice conditions, with a 40-year service life and refuelling intervals of seven years. After the second unit's physical start-up is completed, the vessel will undergo comprehensive testing of its nuclear power plant, including validation of reactor controls, protection systems and monitoring equipment.

This development advances Russia's capability in nuclear-powered maritime transport, a sector where Moscow has invested heavily for Arctic resource development and shipping.
