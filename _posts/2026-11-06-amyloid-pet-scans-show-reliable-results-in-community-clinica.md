A new study published in The Journal of Nuclear Medicine found that local radiologists and nuclear medicine physicians can reliably interpret amyloid positron emission tomography (PET) scans in routine clinical settings, with results showing excellent agreement with specialist expert panels. The research analysed nearly 1,500 scans and demonstrated that visual reads performed in community hospitals and clinics are highly dependable for supporting Alzheimer's disease diagnosis and management. This validation is significant because it broadens access to amyloid imaging as a diagnostic tool, reducing the need for centralised expert review and accelerating clinical decision-making. As amyloid-targeted therapies for Alzheimer's disease expand, reliable local interpretation of imaging will be essential to efficient patient care pathways.
