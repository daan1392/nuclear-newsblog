The International Atomic Energy Agency published a report on isotope-based methods for tracing nitrate pollution in river and groundwater systems, concluding a four-year coordinated research project involving 14 countries.

The participating nations—Argentina, Australia, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ecuador, Finland, France, Greece, Italy, Malaysia, Nigeria, Pakistan, Spain, and Ukraine—collaborated to develop and test new diagnostic techniques. Isotope tracing exploits the distinctive atomic signatures of different nitrogen sources, allowing researchers to distinguish between pollution from agricultural runoff, sewage, and other origins.

The methods offer cost-effective monitoring tools for water resource management and pollution remediation, particularly useful for developing countries managing groundwater quality across large areas. The report documents best practices and technical approaches validated across diverse hydrogeological settings.
