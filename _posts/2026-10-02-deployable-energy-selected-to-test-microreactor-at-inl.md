Idaho National Laboratory named start-up Deployable Energy as the next participant in the Demonstration of Microreactor Experiments (DOME) test bed at the National Reactor Innovation Center (NRIC). The selection positions the company to advance its reactor design toward commercial deployment through hands-on testing and validation.

The DOME facility provides a controlled environment for developers to collect real-world operational data, validate safety systems and de-risk their designs before undertaking formal licensing with the NRC. Access to NRIC's resources is highly competitive and represents an important gate for emerging reactor vendors seeking to bring novel designs to market.

Deployable Energy's selection was unexpected in some quarters, with industry observers having anticipated that established vendor Westinghouse might occupy the next slot. The choice reflects NRIC's focus on supporting diverse advanced reactor concepts and early-stage companies that lack access to other major testing infrastructure. The company joins a growing roster of microreactor and advanced reactor developers working through government-supported pathways to validate designs and accelerate the timeline to commercialisation.
