Westinghouse announced plans to build a new fuel fabrication plant at its Columbia facility in Hopkins, South Carolina, to produce low-enriched uranium plus (LEU+) fuel enriched up to 8 percent U-235. The $1 billion investment will enable annual production of 600 metric tons of uranium fuel beginning in 2030.

The company has already completed pre-construction work on the site and has been coordinating with the Nuclear Regulatory Commission since 2024 ahead of submitting a license amendment application. LEU+ fuel represents a higher enrichment level than traditional low-enriched uranium fuel, offering potential advantages for certain reactor designs and fuel utilization.

This expansion reflects growing demand for diverse fuel types as the nuclear industry pursues new reactor technologies and life-extension programs for existing plants. The facility will serve as a key component of the U.S. fuel supply chain.
