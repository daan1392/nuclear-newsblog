Romania's Cernavodă nuclear power units have resumed grid operation following a three-month shutdown that began in late July when severe drought across central Europe reduced the Danube River to historically low water levels, threatening the plant's ability to supply adequate cooling water. Unit 1 was reconnected to the grid on 6 November, with unit 2 undergoing start-up procedures and expected to synchronise to the grid on 7 November. The incident highlighted climate-related vulnerabilities in nuclear plant siting and operations, particularly for facilities dependent on river cooling in regions facing intensifying drought cycles. Nuclearelectrica and the Romanian government are now developing long-term solutions to prevent recurrence of such disruptions, including potential infrastructure or operational adaptations to sustain output during water stress events.
