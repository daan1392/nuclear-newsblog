Officials in Wisconsin and Vermont have publicly discussed plans to deploy small modular reactors at sites that previously housed conventional large nuclear reactors. In Wisconsin, Dairyland Power Cooperative and nuclear developer Applied Atomics signed a letter of intent to explore SMR feasibility at Dairyland's former La Crosse nuclear power plant site in Genoa.

Former nuclear sites offer significant advantages for SMR deployment: existing nuclear regulatory expertise among local authorities, established emergency preparedness infrastructure, grid connection points, cooling water access, and workforce familiarity with nuclear operations. These factors can reduce development timelines and costs compared to greenfield sites.

SMRs typically produce 50–300 MWe per unit, compared to conventional reactors at 500–1,300 MWe. Their modular design and smaller capital requirements make them suitable for diverse applications including industrial heat, remote locations, and replacing retiring baseload generation. The expressed interest in site reuse reflects growing US momentum around SMR commercialisation.
