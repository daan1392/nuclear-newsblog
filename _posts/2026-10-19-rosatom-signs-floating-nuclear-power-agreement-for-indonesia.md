Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom signed an agreement to supply floating nuclear power units to Indonesia during Russian Energy Week in Moscow. The deal involves deployment of two FPU-100 floating power units to the northern part of Halmahera Island in North Maluku, providing combined capacity of 200 MW to the region.

Rosatom anticipates the units will begin supplying electricity to consumers within five years, pending regulatory approvals and construction timelines. Floating nuclear plants are designed to provide power to remote or island locations where traditional land-based infrastructure proves challenging or uneconomical, offering a potential pathway for decentralized nuclear deployment in developing regions.

The agreement represents expansion of Russian nuclear technology exports to Southeast Asia and signals Indonesia's interest in diversifying its energy supply amid growing electricity demand. Floating power units have been developed by Russia and other nations to address grid infrastructure gaps, though deployment in tropical regions raises questions about operational experience and maintenance capabilities. The deal underscores competition among nuclear-exporting nations for market share in Asia-Pacific energy projects.
