Debate over France's commitment to construct six new EPR2 reactors frequently centers on a €100 per megawatt-hour contract-for-difference (CfD), a figure often conflated with the actual cost of electricity production. Clarification of this distinction is important for informed policy discussion.

A CfD represents a guaranteed revenue floor that investors require to justify capital commitment; it is not equivalent to the production cost, the consumer price, or the state's net expenditure. The €100/MWh CfD level indicates the minimum secured revenue deemed necessary to make the EPR2 investment financially viable under specified assumptions about capital costs, financing terms, and operational parameters.

Actual electricity production costs may be lower or higher than the CfD floor depending on construction performance, financing outcomes, and operational efficiency. Consumer prices depend on market mechanisms and grid supply mix. Government cost depends on how the CfD is structured and whether market revenues exceed the floor. The distinction matters for evaluating whether the EPR2 program represents a cost-effective decarbonization strategy compared to alternatives, and whether implicit subsidies are justified by energy security or climate objectives.
