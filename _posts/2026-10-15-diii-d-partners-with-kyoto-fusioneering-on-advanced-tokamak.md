The DIII-D National Fusion Facility announced a partnership with Kyoto Fusioneering to design, manufacture, install and operate a new power exhaust system in the DIII-D tokamak. The project builds on Kyoto Fusioneering's earlier partnership with Oak Ridge National Laboratory and advances research into negative triangularity plasma configurations—a potentially more stable and efficient approach to magnetic confinement fusion.

Power exhaust systems are critical components in tokamak reactors, removing excess heat from the plasma to prevent damage to the vessel walls. Improving exhaust performance is essential for achieving sustained fusion reactions at reactor-relevant conditions. The DIII-D facility, operated by General Atomics, is a key US research asset for exploring advanced tokamak concepts.

The public-private partnership model enables commercial fusion companies to test and validate technologies at operational research facilities before deploying them in demonstration and commercial reactors. Kyoto Fusioneering's involvement reflects growing commercial momentum in fusion technology development and the importance of bridging research and industry efforts.
