Stellarator developer Thea Energy has announced a collaboration with MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center to test and validate magnet coil technology for its Eos stellarator design. The work will be conducted at MIT's Cambridge facility and focuses on demonstrating the performance of encircling magnet coils under operating conditions.

Stellarators offer an alternative magnetic confinement approach to tokamaks, with potential advantages including steady-state operation and simpler plasma control. Magnet design and coil validation are critical to demonstrating technical and economic feasibility of the approach.

The partnership between a private fusion venture and a leading fusion research center reflects the growing collaboration between commercial fusion companies and academic institutions to advance key enabling technologies. Successful magnet validation reduces technical risk for future prototype construction.
