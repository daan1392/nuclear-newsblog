The Nuclear Regulatory Commission finalized a rule on October 22 that establishes a more direct pathway for commercial reactor applicants to leverage analyses, test data, and technical evidence from reactor demonstrations previously authorized by the Department of Energy or Department of Defense. The measure is intended to accelerate licensing by reducing duplicative safety and technical review.

The new framework allows developers whose designs have already undergone federal scrutiny—whether through DOE demonstration projects or military applications—to reference that existing evidence when pursuing NRC commercial licenses. This approach reduces the need for repetitive testing and analysis while maintaining independent NRC safety oversight.

The rule reflects regulatory recognition that federal demonstration programs have established technical foundations for emerging reactor technologies, including small modular reactors and advanced coolant designs. By creating a more efficient pathway, the NRC aims to reduce licensing timelines and costs for applicants developing follow-on commercial units. The decision is expected to particularly benefit SMR developers and companies deploying variants of previously demonstrated designs, supporting the expanding pipeline of new capacity expected in the late 2020s and 2030s.
