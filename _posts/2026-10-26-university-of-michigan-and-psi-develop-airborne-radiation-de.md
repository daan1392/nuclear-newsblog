Researchers from the University of Michigan and Switzerland's Paul Scherrer Institute published a new airborne gamma-ray spectrometry method in Nature Communications that identifies single or mixed radiation sources, their activity levels, and precise locations. The technique uses Bayesian inference to process real-time measurement data from aircraft, improving speed and accuracy compared to conventional ground-based surveys.

The method directly supports emergency response in radiological hazards scenarios—such as reactor incidents or nuclear weapons detonation. Responders can rapidly determine evacuation zones, shelter-in-place areas, and necessary protective measures including distribution of potassium iodide and other protective equipment.

Airborne detection reduces exposure to response personnel and covers larger areas faster than ground teams. The technique's ability to distinguish source types helps emergency managers prioritize interventions and communicate risks to the public. The research demonstrates how Bayesian computational methods applied to mobile radiation spectrometry can enhance emergency preparedness and radiological consequence management across jurisdictions.
