Researchers at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory have developed a new imaging technique that allows modern PET (positron emission tomography) scanners to visualize targeted alpha therapy (TAT) treatments in cancer patients. The advance uses a novel algorithm to extract diagnostic information from standard scanner equipment.

Targeted alpha therapy delivers alpha particles directly to cancer cells, offering a promising approach for treating certain malignancies. However, clinical adoption has been limited partly by the lack of practical imaging tools to verify therapy delivery and monitor treatment response. Current PET scanners, which are widely installed in hospitals, were not previously capable of imaging the isotopes used in alpha therapy.

By enabling existing scanner infrastructure to image TAT procedures, the new technique could reduce barriers to clinical implementation and broaden access to this emerging cancer treatment modality. The work represents a step toward making advanced radiotherapy techniques more practically available to patients in routine clinical settings.
