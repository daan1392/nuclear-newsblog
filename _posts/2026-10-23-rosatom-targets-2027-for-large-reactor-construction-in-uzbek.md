Rosatom plans to launch construction of two large-scale 1,000 MW units at the proposed Uzbek power plant in 2027, CEO Alexey Likhachev announced during a site visit to the Jizzakh region. The timeline follows ongoing foundation work for two small modular reactors already under construction at the same site, with the concrete foundation slab scheduled for completion by year-end 2026.

The project represents one of the largest international nuclear ventures currently in development, combining both SMR and large reactor technology on a single energy campus. Likhachev characterized the SMR facility as "unique, state-of-the-art" and "the only one in the world currently being implemented using an export model," underscoring Rosatom's positioning of the technology for international markets.

Uzbekistan's pursuit of nuclear power reflects Central Asia's growing electricity demand and industrial development goals. The dual-technology approach—smaller units for initial experience and larger units for substantial capacity—offers a phased entry into nuclear generation while managing financial and technical risk. The project demonstrates continued Russian engagement in civilian nuclear exports despite geopolitical tensions, and provides a reference point for other nations evaluating Russian reactor designs and project execution capabilities.
