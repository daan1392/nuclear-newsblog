Google and Constellation Energy announced a 20-year power purchase agreement to support 890 MW of new nuclear capacity from uprates across 11 reactors in the PJM Interconnection region, spanning Illinois, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey. Constellation will invest $4.3 billion in the upgrade projects, with completion targeted by the end of 2032.

The deal represents a significant trend in the U.S. nuclear sector: major technology companies committing to long-term nuclear power procurement to secure reliable, low-carbon electricity for data centers and computing infrastructure. This follows similar corporate offtake agreements with other utilities, signaling growing appetite from hyperscalers for existing nuclear capacity.

For Constellation, one of the largest nuclear operators in the United States, the agreement provides financial certainty for capital-intensive modernisation programmes. For the grid, the uprates will deliver additional capacity without requiring new generation sources or extensive transmission upgrades.
