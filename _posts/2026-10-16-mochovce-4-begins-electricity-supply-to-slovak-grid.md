Slovakia's Mochovce 4, a 471 MWe VVER-440 pressurised water reactor, was connected to the national grid and began supplying electricity on October 15, marking a significant milestone for the country's energy infrastructure. The unit was brought online at 20% power output, with Slovenske elektrárne confirming that its generation capacity equals approximately 13% of Slovakia's total electricity consumption.

Mochovce 4 is the fourth unit at the Mochovce site and represents the culmination of years of construction and commissioning work. The phasing-in of the reactor to the electricity system proceeded under controlled conditions as engineers ramped up power gradually. Branislav Strýček, Chairman and CEO of Slovenske elektrárne, called the moment historic, noting that generations of workers had prepared for this stage.

The reactor's connection underscores progress in Central European nuclear expansion amid regional efforts to secure carbon-free electricity supplies and reduce dependence on fossil fuel imports.
