Oklo revealed plans for Groves-2, the next phase of its Groves advanced reactor project, with initial focus on medical isotope production. The project builds on progress achieved through the Department of Energy's Reactor Pilot Program and will be supported by the DOE's follow-on Nuclear Energy Launch Pad Program.

Production of medical isotopes—radioactive elements used in imaging and cancer therapy—is an emerging application for advanced reactors. Research reactors and cyclotrons currently supply most medical isotope demand, but capacity constraints and aging facilities have prompted interest in using commercial advanced reactors for isotope production. Oklo's focus on this market segment reflects recognition that flexible, smaller reactors can serve niche energy and isotope production needs.

The announcement demonstrates how private advanced reactor developers are exploring multiple revenue streams beyond baseload electricity generation. Medical isotope production could provide near-term cash flow and operational experience while commercial electricity markets develop.
