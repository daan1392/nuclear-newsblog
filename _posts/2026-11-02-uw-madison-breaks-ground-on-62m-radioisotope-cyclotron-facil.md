The University of Wisconsin–Madison School of Medicine and Public Health has begun construction on a facility to house two cyclotrons aimed at significantly expanding domestic radioisotope production. The $62 million project, funded by the National Institutes of Health, the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation, and private donations, will increase the university's radioisotope production capacity by two- to eightfold depending on the isotope type.

The 13,000-square-foot facility, located within the Wisconsin Institutes for Medical Research complex, is scheduled for completion by May 2028. A key feature is what the university describes as "the first modern alpha-particle cyclotron of its kind installed in the United States." The alpha-particle cyclotron enables production of alpha-emitting isotopes used in targeted radiotherapy, a growing area in nuclear medicine for cancer treatment.

This project addresses a long-standing gap in U.S. capacity for producing medical-grade radioisotopes domestically, reducing reliance on foreign sources and supporting both research and clinical applications.
