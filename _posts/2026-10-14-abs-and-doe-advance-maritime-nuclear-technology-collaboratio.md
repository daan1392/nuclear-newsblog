The American Bureau of Shipping (ABS) and the U.S. Department of Energy's Office of Nuclear Energy (DOE-NE) signed a memorandum of understanding to advance modern commercial nuclear technology applications at sea, including floating nuclear power plants, nuclear-powered vessels, and port and shipyard infrastructure.

The collaboration signals official interest in marine applications of nuclear technology—an area with potential for decarbonizing shipping, powering remote operations, and providing grid support in coastal regions. Floating power plants could serve regions with limited land availability or where grid infrastructure is underdeveloped. Nuclear-powered cargo and container ships could substantially reduce maritime emissions while supporting climate targets in the shipping sector.

ABS, a major international classification society overseeing marine vessel standards and safety, provides critical expertise in vessel design, construction oversight, and regulatory compliance. Its partnership with DOE-NE indicates serious technical and policy engagement with nuclear maritime concepts. Challenges ahead include developing safety frameworks specific to marine environments, supply chain readiness, and demonstrating economic viability compared to alternative fuels and propulsion systems.
