The Philippines is advancing plans for nuclear power to address rapid electricity demand growth, with a target of 1.2 GW of installed capacity by 2032 and longer-term ambitions to reach 4.8 GW by 2050, representatives announced at the World Nuclear Supply Chain conference in Manila.

The country is working to establish the policy, regulatory, and commercial foundations necessary for its first nuclear power project. This represents a significant policy shift for the Philippines, which has not previously operated commercial nuclear reactors. The timeline reflects a staged approach: near-term development focuses on establishing the enabling environment, including regulatory infrastructure and public acceptance work.

The Philippines joins other Southeast Asian nations in reassessing nuclear energy as part of strategies to meet growing electricity demand while enhancing energy security and diversifying the generation mix. Regional developments suggest nuclear is gaining consideration among ASEAN countries as they balance climate goals with grid reliability and economic development needs.
