EDF outlined its strategy to accelerate EPR2 construction by targeting a duration of 70 months per reactor, significantly shorter than the current pace of major new builds. The utility assembled 35 companies and conducted roughly 150 workshops to identify 29 major optimisation pathways across site organisation, prefabrication, assembly, and logistics—all now integrated into the EPR2 programme.

EDF drew lessons from Hinkley Point C in the U.K. and recent Chinese nuclear construction projects to refine methods for managing large, complex worksites. Preparatory work at Penly began in 2024 and is advancing rapidly, with first concrete targeted for 2029 and service entry around 2036–2037. Gravelines received construction authorisation in August 2026.

These efforts reflect efforts across the nuclear sector to reduce construction timelines and costs through enhanced project management, modular assembly techniques, and supply chain integration—essential for scaling new reactor deployment across Europe.
