Westinghouse Electric Company announced a $1 billion investment in the Columbia Fuel Fabrication Facility in Hopkins, South Carolina, the first new fleet-scale nuclear fuel factory to be built in the United States in more than 50 years.

The facility will produce LEU+ fuel, which features higher enrichment levels than the low-enriched uranium (LEU) fuel currently used in most commercial reactors. LEU+ is designed to enhance reactor performance and efficiency. Westinghouse aims to begin operations in 2030, with lead test assemblies using this fuel type already deployed in a commercial US reactor.

The investment reflects confidence in sustained demand for nuclear fuel as the industry expands. A functioning domestic fuel supply chain is critical infrastructure for the sector, reducing reliance on foreign suppliers and ensuring fuel security for US reactors. This development also supports the broader fuel cycle ecosystem as new reactor deployments and life extensions increase overall fuel requirements.
