Extending the operating lives of existing nuclear plants delivers the cheapest electricity of any technology studied in a new report from the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency (NEA) and the Electric Power Research Institute (EPRI).

The report compares the levelized cost of electricity (LCOE), a measure of average lifetime cost per unit of electricity generated, across 23 technologies. It concluded that diversifying power generation to include new and lower-carbon options points to more expensive electricity overall.

Long-term operation of existing nuclear plants is an exception to that trend. Because their initial investment costs have been amortised, such plants have the lowest mean and median LCOE of any technology analysed.

The finding supports the economic case for lifetime extensions and uprates. LCOE does not capture every system factor, such as grid integration or firm-capacity value, so it is best read as one input into planning rather than a complete comparison.
