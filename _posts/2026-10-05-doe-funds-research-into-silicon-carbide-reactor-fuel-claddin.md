The U.S. Department of Energy's Office of Nuclear Energy announced a $50 million funding opportunity to advance research and development of silicon carbide (SiC) cladding for nuclear reactor fuel.

Silicon carbide is emerging as a promising advanced fuel cladding material capable of withstanding extreme neutron, thermal, and chemical conditions within reactor cores. Enhanced cladding performance can improve reactor safety margins and operational efficiency while potentially reducing operational costs across the existing and future reactor fleet.

The funding initiative aims to accelerate the transition of SiC cladding technology from laboratory demonstration toward near-term commercial deployment. Development of accident-tolerant fuels and improved cladding systems is a priority for the U.S. nuclear industry and regulatory framework, supporting both extended operation of current reactors and advanced reactor designs.

This investment reflects DOE's commitment to strengthening nuclear technology fundamentals and supporting sustained advancement of the domestic nuclear fuel supply chain.
