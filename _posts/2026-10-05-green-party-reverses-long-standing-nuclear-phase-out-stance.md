Members of the Green Party of England and Wales voted at their autumn conference to remove nuclear from a list of energy sources to be phased out, marking a significant policy reversal for the environmentalist party.

The motion eliminated language describing nuclear alongside coal and waste incineration as technologies requiring phase-out, replacing it with wording that reserves phase-out status for energy forms incompatible with renewable and low-carbon goals—explicitly excluding fossil fuels. The change reflects growing recognition among climate advocates that nuclear power is essential to decarbonization.

This shift aligns with broader trends across Europe and beyond, where environmental concerns about climate change have led formerly anti-nuclear parties and organisations to reconsider the technology's role in displacing fossil fuels. The timing is significant as the UK advances plans for new nuclear capacity and faces mounting pressure to eliminate emissions while maintaining reliable electricity supply.

The policy update removes a longstanding barrier to nuclear development in British political discourse, potentially facilitating greater consensus around low-carbon energy policy.
