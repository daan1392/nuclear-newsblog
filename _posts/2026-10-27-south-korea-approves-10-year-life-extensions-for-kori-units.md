South Korea's Nuclear Safety and Security Commission approved a 10-year operating extension for Kori Nuclear Power Plant's units 3 and 4, allowing the Westinghouse pressurised water reactors to continue operation until 2034 and 2035 respectively. Both 1,025 MWe units, located near Busan, had completed their original 40-year design lives—unit 3 in September 2024 and unit 4 in August 2025.

Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power plans to restart the units during the first half of 2027 after extended outages. The regulatory approval follows comprehensive safety evaluations demonstrating the units' fitness for continued service. Life extensions are an important part of South Korea's strategy to maintain baseload generation capacity while new reactors enter service.

The decision reflects broader regional trends in extending the operational lives of existing reactors to support decarbonisation goals and grid reliability, particularly in countries with high electricity demand and limited alternative low-carbon generation sources.
