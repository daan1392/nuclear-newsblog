The International Atomic Energy Agency published a report describing outcomes from a four-year coordinated research project on isotope-based methods for tracing nitrate pollution in river and groundwater systems. The project involved 14 participating countries: Argentina, Australia, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Ecuador, Finland, France, Greece, Italy, Malaysia, Nigeria, Pakistan, Spain, and Ukraine.

Isotope tracing exploits stable and radioactive isotope signatures to identify pollution sources and track contaminant transport through water bodies. Nitrate from agricultural runoff, wastewater discharge, and atmospheric deposition carries distinct isotopic fingerprints depending on origin, allowing differentiation of pollution sources and assessment of their relative contributions to contaminated aquifers and surface waters.

The research developed standardized methodologies applicable across diverse hydrogeological settings and climates, enhancing capabilities for water resource protection and remediation. Isotope tracing complements conventional water quality monitoring and provides cost-effective tools for developing nations addressing agricultural and industrial water pollution.
