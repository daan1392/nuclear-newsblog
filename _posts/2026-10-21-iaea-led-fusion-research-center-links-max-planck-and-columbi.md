The Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics (IPP) in Germany and Columbia University launched the Max Planck Columbia Center, a virtual joint research facility directed by IPP director Thomas Klinger and Columbia associate professor Carlos Paz-Soldan. The center aims to advance magnetic confinement fusion research through coordinated transatlantic collaboration.

The partnership leverages complementary capabilities: IPP's decades of experience with large tokamak and stellarator research programs, including the ASDEX Upgrade tokamak, and Columbia's emerging fusion research infrastructure and academic resources. Paz-Soldan also directs Columbia's own Fusion Research Center, creating an integrated transatlantic network. The virtual structure enables collaborative research, student exchanges, and shared data access without requiring new physical infrastructure.

Magnetic confinement fusion remains scientifically challenging despite significant progress over the past decade. Increased international collaboration, particularly between leading research institutions in Europe and North America, reflects recognition that advancing toward commercial fusion requires sustained investment in fundamental science. The center's establishment signals confidence in long-term fusion prospects and positions participating institutions at the forefront of public-sector magnetic fusion research.
