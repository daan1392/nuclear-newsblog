The French Academy of Technologies issued a recommendation in September 2026 calling on France and Europe to prepare strategy for fusion development beyond the International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor, emphasizing the need for early action on technological gaps.

The Academy's opinion highlighted a key challenge: while ITER will achieve critical scientific milestones, the pathway from scientific demonstration to commercial fusion electricity remains unclear. The Academy recommended prioritization of developing a volumetric neutron source—a technology enabling efficient breeding of fusion fuel and materials testing at scales currently unavailable.

The statement reflects recognition that global fusion competition is accelerating, with multiple national programmes and private ventures pursuing diverse technical approaches. Europe risks falling behind unless strategic investments in critical technologies are initiated now, during the ITER construction and early operational phase.

The recommendation addresses a broader concern across the European fusion community: ITER, while scientifically valuable, is not itself a demonstration plant or path to commercialization. Securing Europe's long-term fusion capacity requires parallel programmes addressing practical engineering and industrial questions that ITER does not answer.
