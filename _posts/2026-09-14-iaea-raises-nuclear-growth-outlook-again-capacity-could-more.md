The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) raised its long-term nuclear power projections for the sixth consecutive year, and now says global capacity could more than triple by 2060.

In its report *Energy, Electricity and Nuclear Power Estimates for the Period up to 2060*, the agency said 413 reactors were operable worldwide at the end of 2025, with a combined capacity of 377.1 GWe. Its high-case scenario sees that rising to 1,045 GWe by 2050.

Small modular reactors (SMRs), which are factory-built units of modest output, feature prominently. In the high case, SMRs could supply as much as 284 GWe by 2060. That is equivalent to about 946 units if they average 300 MWe each, or about 1,136 units at an average of 250 MWe.

The figures are scenarios, not forecasts of what will be built. The high case depends on policy support, financing and supply-chain expansion. The report was released during the 70th IAEA General Conference in Vienna, where expanding nuclear capacity has been a central theme.
