The US Department of Energy's Advanced Research Projects Agency-Energy selected eight nuclear fuel technology projects to share up to $50 million in federal funding through its High-performance Optimised Recycled Nuclear Isotopes for Gen IV reactors (HORNIG) programme. The initiative, which opened for applications in April, supports research and development of commercially deployable technologies that recycle transuranic elements—heavy uranium isotopes left in spent fuel—into fissile materials suitable for next-generation reactors. This approach addresses both inventory management of existing used fuel and fuel supply challenges for advanced reactor designs. The HORNIG programme builds on prior Department of Energy investments in fuel recycling research, reflecting growing interest in closing the fuel cycle to maximize resource efficiency and reduce long-term waste storage burdens as the nation considers expanded nuclear capacity.
