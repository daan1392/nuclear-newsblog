The International Atomic Energy Agency has launched the Global Network of Isotopes in Lakes (GNIL), a collaborative initiative to monitor stable isotope composition and water chemistry in lakes worldwide. The network will establish an international database of isotopic measurements, chemical data, and environmental observations to help countries assess freshwater ecosystem health and prioritise conservation and restoration efforts. Lakes serve billions of people by supplying drinking water, food, and economic benefits, yet freshwater systems face unprecedented pressure from degradation and climate impacts. By using stable isotope analysis—a non-radioactive tracer technique—participating countries can track changes in water circulation, precipitation patterns, and ecosystem conditions. This approach enables evidence-based management of increasingly stressed freshwater resources.
