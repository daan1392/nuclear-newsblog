PET/MRI can pinpoint the brain regions responsible for seizures in children with difficult-to-diagnose epilepsy, even when MRI scans are negative or inconclusive, according to a new study.

The work examined a large paediatric cohort that included children younger than 6. PET/MRI combines positron emission tomography, which shows metabolic or molecular activity, with MRI's anatomical detail in a single scan. The researchers found that combining PET/MRI findings with EEG (electrical brain recording) results and a structured presurgical evaluation improved identification of seizure-causing tissue. It also informed treatment decisions.

The study was published in the August issue of The Journal of Nuclear Medicine. Locating the seizure focus is a key step in deciding whether a child may benefit from epilepsy surgery, and cases where conventional MRI shows nothing abnormal are especially hard. The findings support a role for hybrid molecular imaging in such cases.
