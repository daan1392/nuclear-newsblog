Uranium Energy Corporation announced the formation of a National Security Advisory Council comprising four senior former U.S. officials to guide its newly established United States Uranium Refining & Conversion Corporation (UR&C) subsidiary. The appointees include two retired Navy admirals—James F. Caldwell, former director of the Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program, and William K. Lescher, former vice chief of naval operations—as well as Tom D'Agostino and Neile Miller, both former leaders of the National Nuclear Security Administration.

The advisory council's composition underscores the strategic importance the U.S. government places on domestic uranium conversion capacity. Conversion—the process of transforming raw uranium into forms suitable for enrichment and fuel fabrication—has long been concentrated outside the United States, creating supply chain vulnerabilities that policymakers seek to address.

The advisory structure suggests UR&C aims to position itself for government contracts and policy support as the U.S. prioritizes fuel cycle resilience. High-level expertise in naval propulsion and nuclear security reflects the civilian nuclear industry's deep ties to defense and strategic infrastructure concerns.
