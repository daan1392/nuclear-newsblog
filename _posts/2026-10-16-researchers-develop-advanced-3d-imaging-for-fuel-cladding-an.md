Researchers at Switzerland's Paul Scherrer Institute published a study describing ptychographic X-ray computed tomography (PXCT), a nondestructive 3D imaging technique for assessing fuel rod cladding structural integrity. The method, developed at PSI's Swiss Light Source facility, creates high-resolution images of complex materials without requiring sample preparation or sectioning.

Zirconium alloy cladding is critical to reactor safety and performance, containing fission products and maintaining pressure boundary integrity during operation and accident scenarios. Traditional examination methods, including electron microscopy, required destructive sample preparation. The PXCT technique preserves specimens for subsequent analysis or archival storage.

The advancement supports improved understanding of cladding performance under reactor conditions and accident scenarios, informing materials selection, design optimization, and regulatory safety assessments. The technique's ability to examine multiple samples nondestructively may accelerate materials research supporting advanced reactor designs and extended fuel cycles.
