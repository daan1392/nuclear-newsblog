Nuclear reactors worldwide generated 2,702 TWh of electricity in 2025, a new record that beat the 2,667 TWh set the year before, according to the World Nuclear Association's World Nuclear Outlook Report.

The report also assessed progress toward the goal of tripling global nuclear capacity by 2050, which 38 countries have backed. It calculated that if every national nuclear target were achieved, total capacity would rise to more than three times today's level by 2050. A related investment guide from the association puts the upper scenario, including operable, planned and proposed reactors plus government targets, at 1,446 GWe, against about 403 GWe of operable reactors today.

Looking at the existing fleet, the report noted a recent increase in construction starts. Starts have fluctuated considerably over the past 75 years. It also sets out a series of policy recommendations aimed at helping the tripling ambition be met.

The report's author, Jonathan Cobb, discussed the findings in depth on the World Nuclear News podcast. The headline numbers indicate steady growth from the existing fleet. The large gap between current capacity and the target scenario shows how much new build, and financing, would still be needed.
