The city of Dryden in western Ontario voted to formally request that Canada's Major Projects Office deny "project of national interest" status for a proposed deep geological repository for nuclear waste sited nearby. The designation affects project review and federal jurisdiction.

Simultaneously, Canada's Federal Court of Appeal ruled that the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission did not have an expanded duty to consult the Kebaowek First Nation regarding construction of the Near Surface Disposal Facility (NSDF) near Chalk River Laboratories, approximately 200 kilometers northwest of Ottawa. The court upheld the CNSC's initial approval of the NSDF site.

These developments highlight ongoing tensions surrounding waste management site selection in Canada. While the NSDF received regulatory approval and court backing, local opposition to the broader deep repository project persists. Managing stakeholder concerns and Indigenous consultation remains central to Canada's efforts to establish permanent disposal solutions for legacy nuclear waste.
