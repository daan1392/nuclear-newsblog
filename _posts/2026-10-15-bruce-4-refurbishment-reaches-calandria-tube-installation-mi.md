Installation of calandria tubes at Bruce unit 4 in Ontario was completed faster than at the previous unit, Bruce 3, as the Major Component Replacement (MCR) project continues to set new records for CANDU reactor refurbishment. The calandria tube is a critical component in CANDU reactors, made of Zircaloy (a zirconium alloy), that forms the outer boundary of the fuel channel and contains pressurized heavy water moderator.

Bruce 4 is the third of six CANDU units at the Ontario site undergoing MCR, a multi-year, multi-billion dollar undertaking that extends reactor operating life by 30 to 35 years. The project demonstrates the technical and economic viability of extending large reactor lifetimes through systematic replacement of critical pressure boundary and other major components.

Improving refurbishment efficiency and cost has strategic importance for countries with aging reactor fleets seeking to maintain operating capacity while new units are constructed. Bruce 4's progress suggests that industrialization and learning from successive MCR projects are reducing both schedule and cost per unit.
