Ghana's Foreign Ministry announced that Russia has secured "concrete commitments" to assist with the African nation's first nuclear power plant, a planned 2,000-MW facility. Ghana's nuclear energy ambitions are not new—in the 1960s, President Kwame Nkrumah initiated nuclear plans that were cut short by political upheaval.

The renewed commitment reflects broader international interest in nuclear power as a low-carbon energy source for developing economies. According to reports, construction could begin by 2027, though the initial announcement provided limited technical details on reactor type, financing arrangements, or project timeline.

This agreement marks Russia's expanding involvement in African nuclear development and signals Ghana's determination to diversify its electricity generation mix and reduce carbon emissions. The project remains subject to regulatory approvals and feasibility assessments.
