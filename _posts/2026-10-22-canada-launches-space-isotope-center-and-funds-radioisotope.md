Canada's Nuclear Innovation Institute launched the Canadian Centre for Space Isotopes, a new national facility supporting development and deployment of radioisotope power systems and thermal technologies for space exploration. Concurrently, the Canadian Space Agency announced C$11.1 million (approximately $7.9 million USD) in funding distributed among 16 space projects, with two specifically targeting radioisotope technologies. These initiatives reflect growing recognition of radioisotopes' strategic importance for deep-space missions and remote operations where conventional power sources are impractical. Radioisotope thermoelectric generators have proven essential for decades-long planetary missions. Canada's coordinated investment in both infrastructure and targeted research projects positions the country to develop and commercialize advanced isotope-based space power and propulsion systems.
