Thorizon, a molten salt reactor (MSR) developer, and TNO, a Dutch research and technology organisation, launched a joint materials and corrosion programme to generate experimental data and predictive models required for material qualification in molten salt reactors. The programme addresses a critical barrier to MSR commercialisation: understanding how structural materials behave over decades of exposure to hot, corrosive molten salt environments.

Molten salt reactors operate with fuel salt and coolant salt at temperatures of 600–750°C, creating significant materials challenges. Traditional reactor materials designed for aqueous coolants may not survive the chemical and thermal conditions in MSR systems. Systematic material testing and model development are essential to ensure safe, long-term operation and to support licensing and commercial deployment.

Thorizon's collaboration with TNO leverages Dutch expertise in materials science and thermal hydraulics developed through decades of nuclear research. The programme represents the kind of foundational technical work required to move advanced reactor concepts from demonstration to proven, licensable technology—a prerequisite for investor confidence and regulatory approval.
