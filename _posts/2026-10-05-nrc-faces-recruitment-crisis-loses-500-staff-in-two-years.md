The U.S. Government Accountability Office has highlighted serious workforce challenges at the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, revealing that the agency experienced a net loss of approximately 500 employees between July 2024 and June 2026. The report recommends that the NRC establish clear metrics to assess whether recruitment and retention initiatives are meeting current and projected staffing needs.

The staffing crisis reflects competing pressures on the regulator. The agency is simultaneously implementing new executive orders and provisions from the 2024 ADVANCE Act—both aimed at accelerating nuclear deployment—while facing stiff competition from within the nuclear industry itself for technical talent. Private companies developing small modular reactors, fuel cycle services, and other nuclear technologies are actively recruiting the same experts that the NRC requires.

The GAO also urged the NRC to complete development of a strategic workforce planning tool by early 2027, a step essential for predicting and preparing for future skill gaps. Without adequate staffing and foresight, the agency risks compromising its ability to license, inspect, and oversee an expanding fleet of new reactor designs and operations, potentially becoming a bottleneck in nuclear expansion efforts.
