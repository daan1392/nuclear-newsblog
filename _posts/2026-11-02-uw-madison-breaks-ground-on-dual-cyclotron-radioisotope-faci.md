The University of Wisconsin–Madison School of Medicine and Public Health broke ground on a $62 million project to add two cyclotrons to its medical research infrastructure, expected to increase radioisotope production capacity between two- and eightfold depending on the specific isotope. Funding came from the National Institutes of Health, the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation, and private donations. The 13,000-square-foot facility, integrated into the Wisconsin Institutes for Medical Research complex, will include what the university described as the first modern alpha-particle cyclotron of its kind installed in the United States. Completion is targeted for May 2028. The expansion addresses growing demand for medical isotopes in diagnostic imaging and therapeutic applications, supporting both clinical and research needs at the institution and potentially serving broader regional demand.
