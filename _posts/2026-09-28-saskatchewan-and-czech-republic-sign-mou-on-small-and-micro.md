The Canadian province of Saskatchewan and the Czech Republic have signed a memorandum of understanding to facilitate clean energy solutions, including small modular reactors (SMRs) and micro modular reactors (MMRs).

The two partners bring different nuclear positions. Saskatchewan has very large, high-grade uranium mines but no nuclear power reactors. It is working with Ontario, New Brunswick and Alberta on SMR deployment, and Crown utility SaskPower has begun formally evaluating large reactor technologies.

The Czech Republic gets about one-third of its electricity from nuclear power. It operates four VVER-440 units at Dukovany, which started up between 1985 and 1987, and two units at Temelín, which began operating in 2000 and 2002. The country also has plans for new large reactors.

An MoU of this kind is a framework for cooperation and does not itself commit either side to projects or purchases.
