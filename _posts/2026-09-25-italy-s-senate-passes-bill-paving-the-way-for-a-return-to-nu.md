Italy's Senate has approved Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's bill enabling a return to nuclear power, passing it by 81 votes to 51 with seven abstentions.

The legislation gives the government a mandate to build a framework for new nuclear generation in a country that has been without it for about four decades. Environment and Energy Security Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin said it completes a process begun three years ago to explore the potential of the technology.

He described nuclear power as "not a return to the past" and argued that, combined with renewables, it could support energy security and the goal of climate neutrality by 2050.

The vote is one step in a wider wave of European activity on new nuclear. The mandate sets the direction for policy, while the detailed rules and the practical steps toward building reactors remain to be developed.
