Nuclear medicine is expanding its role in Alzheimer's disease management through amyloid and tau PET imaging, enabling clinicians to visualize pathological hallmarks of the condition and guide treatment decisions. Amyloid PET imaging confirms the presence of amyloid protein accumulation and supports decisions about prescribing new anti-amyloid monoclonal antibody drugs, while tau PET imaging provides information about disease progression and patient prognosis.

These imaging biomarkers help clinicians identify patients most likely to benefit from emerging therapies and monitor treatment response. Early selection based on confirmed pathology reduces unnecessary treatment exposure and improves clinical outcomes. As new targeted Alzheimer's therapies advance toward approval, PET imaging becomes increasingly important for patient stratification and therapeutic monitoring.

The convergence of disease-modifying therapeutics and molecular imaging represents a shift toward personalized medicine in neurodegeneration, leveraging nuclear medicine's ability to visualize cellular-level pathology.
