The Nuclear Energy Agency's September 2025 report "The Costs of Generating Electricity 2025" found a median levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) for nuclear of $81 per megawatt-hour across 21 countries and 23 generation technologies. The LCOE metric, which expresses the average cost per unit of electricity produced over a facility's lifetime, enables comparison across different energy sources accounting for capital expenditure, operating costs, and service life.

While nuclear requires substantial upfront investment, the analysis highlights cost competitiveness when considered over extended operational periods—typically 40–60 years for modern reactors. Low variable costs and high capacity factors contribute to this economic profile. The study provides key data for energy policy decisions as governments weigh generation portfolios to meet climate and reliability objectives.

The NEA's cross-country comparison is particularly relevant as many nations contemplate new nuclear builds and life extensions of existing fleets. Economics remain a critical variable in deployment decisions, alongside grid stability, workforce availability, and regulatory frameworks.
