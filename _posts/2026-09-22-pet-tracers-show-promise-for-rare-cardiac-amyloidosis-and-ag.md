Two recent studies point to useful roles for PET/CT radiotracers in diagnosing rare and hard-to-image diseases.

In a clinical trial published in JAMA, a PET/CT radiotracer showed high sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing cardiac amyloidosis. The condition involves abnormal protein build-up in the heart and can lead to heart failure. The summary supplied did not name the tracer.

Separately, research in The Journal of Nuclear Medicine found that renal medullary carcinoma, a rare and aggressive kidney cancer, takes up the widely used tracer 18F-FDG strongly. Other kidney cancers tend to show more variable uptake. FDG PET/CT remained highly effective after systemic treatment and detected additional metastases that conventional anatomical imaging had missed, giving a fuller picture of how far the cancer had spread.

PET (positron emission tomography) uses radioactive tracers to show metabolic or molecular activity, complementing scans that show only structure. Both studies suggest that established imaging methods could sharpen diagnosis and staging in conditions where clinicians have had limited options.
