University of Michigan and Paul Scherrer Institute researchers published a new method for airborne radiation detection that can accurately identify single or mixed radioactive sources, their activity levels, and location. The research, published in Nature Communications under the title "Quantitative Mobile Gamma-Ray Spectrometry through Bayesian Inference," uses advanced statistical analysis to process gamma-ray spectral data collected from aircraft or drones.

The technique is expected to improve emergency response in radiological incidents such as nuclear reactor accidents or radiological dispersal events. By enabling faster and more precise source identification, it allows responders to determine appropriate evacuation zones and protective measures more quickly, including decisions on distribution of potassium iodide and other protective equipment.

The method addresses a longstanding challenge in radiological assessment: distinguishing multiple isotopes and quantifying their contributions under field conditions. Enhanced capability for rapid, accurate source characterization could significantly improve public safety outcomes in nuclear emergencies.
