Nitrogen pollution from wastewater, agricultural runoff and other human sources poses a growing threat to freshwater and coastal ecosystems, fueling algal blooms and degrading water quality globally. The International Atomic Energy Agency emphasized the use of stable isotope analysis to trace nitrogen sources and pathways in contaminated water systems.

Stable isotope tracing exploits naturally occurring variations in nitrogen isotope ratios, which differ depending on emission sources—synthetic fertilizers, organic waste, or atmospheric deposition carry distinct isotopic signatures. By measuring these ratios in water samples, scientists can identify pollution origins and track transport mechanisms, information essential for effective remediation strategies.

The technique demonstrates the broader utility of nuclear and isotopic methods beyond energy and medicine. Stable isotope hydrology has become standard in environmental monitoring, regulatory compliance, and water resource management. As nutrient pollution intensifies under climate change and agricultural intensification, isotopic tools offer cost-effective means to guide policy and restoration efforts in affected regions.
