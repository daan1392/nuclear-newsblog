Munich-based stellarator developer Proxima Fusion and the State of Lower Saxony have signed a memorandum of understanding to build what would be Europe's first large-scale facility for fusion-grade high-temperature superconducting (HTS) tape.

HTS tape is used to wind the powerful magnets that confine plasma in fusion devices such as stellarators. The agreement covers assessing potential sites in Lower Saxony, setting up a milestone-based support and funding structure, easing permitting, and building partnerships with companies, municipalities and research institutions in the state.

About EUR140 million (USD46.5 million) of investment is planned for the first two project phases, running to the end of 2029. Proxima aims to fund the total in equal parts from private capital and public money. Lower Saxony would contribute EUR21 million, just under 30% of the proposed public share.

The plan reflects fusion companies' efforts to secure domestic supply of key components as they look toward commercial plants.
