The UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero directed the country's nuclear regulators to begin a Generic Design Assessment (GDA) of X-energy's Xe-100 high-temperature gas-cooled reactor.

GDA is a voluntary process in which the Office for Nuclear Regulation, the Environment Agency and Natural Resources Wales assess the safety, security and environmental aspects of a reactor design before a specific site is proposed. The direction followed the department's review of the application from X-energy UK Holdings. It came a year to the day after Centrica and X-energy signed a joint development agreement for the Xe-100 in the UK. The partners have named the Hartlepool site, owned by EDF and Centrica, as their preferred first location for a planned fleet of up to 6 GWe.

Separately, the ONR and Environment Agency told ministers what they had already changed, and planned to change, to speed licensing of new technologies and projects. This responds to the Nuclear Regulatory Taskforce led by John Fingleton, whose final report called for a "radical reset" of the regulatory system. The government's March policy paper, *Building Our Nuclear Nation*, set the wider aim of reshaping the regulatory landscape to speed deployment.
