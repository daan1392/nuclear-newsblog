Scientists at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory developed a machine learning algorithm that can detect and estimate past radiation exposure using information from a complete blood count (CBC), one of the most routinely performed clinical blood tests. The tool analyzes patterns in standard blood cell measurements to identify signatures consistent with radiation exposure history.

The approach offers potential advantages for medical surveillance, occupational monitoring and emergency response scenarios. A portable, low-cost method for assessing radiation dose would benefit workers in nuclear facilities, radiotherapy personnel and first responders in radiation incidents. Current dosimetry methods rely primarily on electronic monitors or biological indicators that require specialized sample processing.

The machine learning model trained on blood samples from exposed individuals can identify exposure patterns that would be difficult to detect through conventional analysis. If validated in clinical and occupational settings, this technique could enable rapid screening of populations following radiological accidents or for long-term health monitoring of radiation workers. Further development and validation are needed before clinical deployment.
