Arizona State University, Purdue University, and the University of Tennessee–Knoxville have announced the formation of the New Nuclear Academic Consortium, a collaborative initiative designed to address emerging gaps in the nuclear workforce. The consortium will develop microcredentials and immersive training tools to broaden access to nuclear education across a wider student population.

The U.S. nuclear sector faces significant labour shortages as experienced workers retire and the industry prepares for fleet expansion driven by new reactor deployments and small modular reactor development. Microcredentials—focused, stackable certifications demonstrating competency in specific technical areas—offer a flexible pathway for career transitions and upskilling complementary to traditional four-year degree programs.

By bringing together three major universities with strong nuclear science and engineering programmes, the consortium aims to create scalable, accessible training platforms that prepare workers for roles in operations, maintenance, security, waste management, and other nuclear specialties critical to industry growth.
