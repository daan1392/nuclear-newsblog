The US Department of Energy approved cost-shared funding through its Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (ARDP) for an accelerated final stage of the Molten Chloride Reactor Experiment (MCRE). The project, led by Southern Company and Core Power in collaboration with Idaho National Laboratory, aims to achieve criticality as early as 2027—a potential world first for a fast-spectrum molten chloride salt reactor.

Molten salt reactors operate at high temperatures with liquid salt as both coolant and fuel medium, offering advantages in thermal efficiency and accident tolerance. Fast-spectrum variants, which use high-energy neutrons, can breed uranium and reduce the radiotoxicity of long-lived waste products. The MCRE will generate critical data on fuel behavior, reactor operation and performance to support future commercial deployment.

The accelerated timeline reflects DOE commitment to demonstrating advanced reactor technologies that could provide flexible, decarbonized electricity and process heat. Successfully achieving criticality would validate decades of molten salt reactor research and position the technology for commercial demonstration in the following decade.
