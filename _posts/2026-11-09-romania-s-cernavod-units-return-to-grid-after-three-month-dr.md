Romania's Cernavodă nuclear units returned to operation after an extended shutdown caused by historically low water levels in the Danube River. Unit 1 was reconnected to the grid on 6 November, with unit 2 synchronised on 7 November, ending an approximately three-month outage that began in late July and early August.

The facility requires substantial cooling water from the Danube and had shut down when the river's water level dropped below safe operating minimums during central Europe's drought. The incident highlighted climate-related vulnerabilities affecting large water-cooled reactor facilities across the continent.

Nuclearelectrica and the Romanian government are developing long-term solutions to prevent future disruptions, including potential infrastructure modifications to ensure reliable cooling water availability during periods of low river flow. The incident underscores the importance of climate adaptation planning for nuclear facilities, particularly in regions where water availability is climate-sensitive.
