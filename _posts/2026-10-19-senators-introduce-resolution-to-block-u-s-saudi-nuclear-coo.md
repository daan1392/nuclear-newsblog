U.S. Senators Jeff Merkley (D-Oregon) and Edward Markey (D-Massachusetts) introduced a Joint Resolution of Disapproval (JRD) in an effort to block the proposed 123 Agreement for civil nuclear cooperation between the United States and Saudi Arabia. The resolution represents the latest congressional pushback against the bilateral nuclear agreement.

Under U.S. law, 123 Agreements—named after Section 123 of the Atomic Energy Act—governing peaceful nuclear cooperation must be approved by Congress. A JRD allows Congress to reject the agreement by vote if it objects to the terms. The introduction of this resolution signals sustained legislative skepticism regarding nuclear cooperation with Saudi Arabia.

Concerns have centered on nonproliferation safeguards, transparency in uranium enrichment capabilities and alignment with U.S. strategic interests in the Middle East. Supporters of the agreement argue it strengthens bilateral relations and channels nuclear development toward civilian applications, while opponents contend that stronger guarantees are needed before U.S. technology and materials transfers proceed. Congressional review remains an active arena for nuclear diplomacy and nonproliferation policy.
