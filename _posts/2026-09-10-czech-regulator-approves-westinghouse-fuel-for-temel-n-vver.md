The Czech State Office for Nuclear Security has approved the use of Westinghouse fuel at ČEZ's VVER-1000 units at the Temelín nuclear power plant.

The new fuel assemblies are due to be loaded into unit 1 during its planned shutdown later this year. They were delivered last year.

ČEZ began diversifying its nuclear fuel suppliers in 2018. In 2022 it signed a contract for Temelín fuel with Westinghouse, and one with France's Framatome. Approval was a long process, involving a five-year series of analyses and tests to ensure the fuel meets strict safety requirements. Experts spent two years alone assessing, from a safety perspective, how the fuel behaves in normal operation.

The decision is a step in reducing reliance on a single fuel vendor for Russian-designed reactors, a common goal in operators of VVER plants in Europe.
