The University of California–Berkeley commissioned a forced-flow molten salt test facility developed by professor Guanyu Su, capable of circulating FLiNaK—a mixture of lithium fluoride, sodium fluoride, and potassium fluoride—under conditions representative of molten-salt reactor operations.

The facility operates between 500°C and 700°C, achieves flow rates up to 60 litres per minute, and maintains temperature differences of up to 100 K between hot and cold legs. These parameters simulate conditions encountered in many proposed molten-salt nuclear reactor designs.

Molten-salt reactors are a category of advanced reactor receiving growing attention for their potential advantages in safety, fuel efficiency, and proliferation resistance. Development of these systems requires extensive testing of salt chemistry, corrosion behaviour, heat transfer, and system integration at realistic operating scales before commercial deployment.

The Berkeley facility supports the broader research ecosystem advancing molten-salt reactor technology, providing data and experience that inform private sector and national laboratory development efforts.
