The starboard reactor unit on the new Chukotka nuclear icebreaker achieved minimum controlled power level, marking the first physical startup of the vessel's two RITM-200 reactors. Part of Russia's Project 22220 fleet, the Chukotka will eventually operate two reactors producing 175 megawatts of thermal power each, designed for a 40-year service life with refueling every seven years.

Each reactor unit weighs over 147 tonnes and measures 7.3 metres tall with a 3.3-metre diameter, engineered for harsh Arctic conditions and extreme vessel rolling. After the second unit achieves criticality, the vessel will undergo comprehensive testing of the nuclear power plant's reactor control, protection, and monitoring systems.

The startup continues Russia's sustained program of nuclear icebreaker development for Arctic maritime operations. These vessels support resource extraction, shipping, and geopolitical presence in polar regions where extreme ice conditions demand continuous high-power propulsion. The two-reactor design provides redundancy and operational flexibility for extended Arctic deployment.
