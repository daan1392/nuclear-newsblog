Quantum Leap Energy, an ASP Isotopes subsidiary, and Necsa, South Africa's state-owned nuclear energy corporation, formed a joint venture to develop and operate high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) production facilities at Necsa's Pelindaba site. The partnership will design, finance, construct and operate both test bench and commercial-scale production infrastructure.

HALEU—uranium enriched to 5–20 percent uranium-235—is essential fuel for advanced reactor designs including many small modular reactors and some high-temperature gas-cooled reactors. Current global HALEU supply is limited, constraining deployment of next-generation reactor technologies. South Africa possesses historical enrichment expertise and infrastructure at Pelindaba, offering potential for regional production capacity.

The joint venture leverages South Africa's nuclear heritage and ASP Isotopes' commercial development experience to establish reliable HALEU supply. Expansion of HALEU production capacity outside traditional suppliers supports diversification of fuel supply chains and enables accelerated deployment of advanced reactor projects globally. The initiative reflects growing strategic emphasis on fuel cycle security among nations pursuing nuclear expansion.
