The Nuclear Regulatory Commission published an inspection report concluding that employees in the supply chain and quality assurance departments at Palisades may be hesitant to raise safety concerns through available channels due to fear of retaliation. The findings stem from a July 2026 inspection at the Covert, Michigan plant, which is attempting to become the first U.S. reactor to restart after permanent shutdown.

Inspectors identified "possible concerns with the safety-conscious work environment (SCWE)" at the facility. A strong safety-conscious work environment is essential for nuclear safety culture—it ensures that workers and engineers feel empowered to identify hazards, challenge unsafe practices, and escalate concerns without fear of adverse consequences. Weaknesses in SCWE have been cited in previous nuclear incidents and are a priority focus for regulators.

The finding comes as Palisades prepares for restart under new ownership by Holtec International. The NRC sent its findings to Holtec's senior leadership in October, and the company will be expected to develop and implement corrective actions. Addressing workplace culture issues is critical to demonstrating operational readiness and building public and regulatory confidence.
