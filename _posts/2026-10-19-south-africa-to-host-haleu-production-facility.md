A joint venture between ASP Isotopes subsidiary Quantum Leap Energy and South African state-owned corporation Necsa will develop, finance, build and operate test bench and commercial-scale facilities for high-assay low-enriched uranium production at Necsa's Pelindaba site.

HALEU—uranium enriched to levels between 5% and 20%—is increasingly important for advanced reactor designs including high-temperature gas coolers and fast reactors. Expanding HALEU production capacity outside traditional enrichment centers addresses supply constraints that could limit deployment of next-generation reactor technologies.

The South African facility positions the country as a potential HALEU supplier for global markets, leveraging Necsa's existing nuclear infrastructure and enrichment expertise. The partnership model—combining private sector development with state-owned capabilities—reflects an approach several nations are adopting to build out fuel cycle capabilities.
