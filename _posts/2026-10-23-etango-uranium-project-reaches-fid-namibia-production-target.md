The Etango Joint Venture—held 55% by Bannerman Energy and 45% by CNNC Overseas Limited—reached a positive final investment decision, enabling the project in Namibia's Erongo Region to proceed to full-scale construction. Commissioning and commercial production are targeted for 2028. The project holds an estimated mineral resource of 207 million pounds U3O8 (approximately 79,600 tonnes of uranium), positioning Etango as a significant global supply source.

Located 30 kilometers southeast of Swakopmund, the Etango site represents one of the largest undeveloped uranium assets outside Kazakhstan and Australia. The FID completion signals growing capital markets confidence in uranium demand and reflects both improved market fundamentals and persistent supply constraints as reactors globally operate at high capacity factors and new builds enter service.

Namibia's uranium sector, historically dependent on the Rossing mine, stands to diversify production with Etango's entry. The project also represents a partnership model between Western and Chinese nuclear fuel interests, reflecting broader internationalization of the uranium supply chain. With multiple new mining and conversion projects advancing globally, Etango's progression contributes to meeting medium-term fuel demand anticipated from reactor restarts and new unit startups.
