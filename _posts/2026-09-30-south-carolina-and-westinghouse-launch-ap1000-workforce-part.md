The University of South Carolina and Westinghouse Electric Company formalized a partnership in late September 2026 to create a workforce development program focused on deploying Westinghouse AP1000 pressurized water reactors. The agreement was unveiled at the Third Annual South Carolina Nuclear Summit, held September 28–29 in Columbia.

The collaboration reflects the practical challenge facing the nuclear sector as new reactor builds accelerate: securing sufficient skilled personnel across construction, operations, and maintenance. By establishing coordinated training pathways through a university partner, Westinghouse aims to build a pipeline of qualified workers ready to support AP1000 projects.

South Carolina has become a regional hub for nuclear development and manufacturing. Extending educational and training infrastructure strengthens the state's position as a center of nuclear excellence and facilitates the large-scale deployment required to meet U.S. energy and decarbonization goals.
