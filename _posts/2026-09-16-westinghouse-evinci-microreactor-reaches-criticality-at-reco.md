Westinghouse completed zero-power, high-temperature criticality testing of its eVinci microreactor, reaching criticality at 663 degrees Celsius.

The work was done with Los Alamos National Laboratory and the Nevada National Security Site at the National Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC) in Nevada, part of the US National Nuclear Security Administration. The US Department of Energy said the testing was also carried out in collaboration with Idaho National Laboratory.

Researchers also took a subcritical reactivity measurement at a peak core temperature of 1,011 degrees C, which Westinghouse said was a record high for a reactivity measurement at NCERC. Zero-power criticality means the core sustains a chain reaction at negligible power, allowing physics behaviour to be studied without significant heat generation.

The temperature-dependent data are intended to strengthen the analytical models used in the design. Westinghouse called the tests a defining milestone in its build-test-learn development approach.
