Los Alamos National Laboratory has developed an electrochemical system for semiautonomous decontamination of radiological gloveboxes used in plutonium operations. Gloveboxes represent a significant decommissioning challenge: disposing of them as transuranic (TRU) waste is orders of magnitude more expensive than low-level waste disposal and consumes limited capacity at the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant in Carlsbad, New Mexico.

Conventional glovebox decontamination requires operators to manually spray and wipe interior surfaces through glove ports, a time-consuming and labor-intensive process. The electrochemical approach offers potential for more efficient, safer decontamination to low-level waste disposal limits, significantly reducing lifecycle costs.

This innovation addresses a practical problem faced across the DOE complex and international nuclear facilities where contaminated gloveboxes must eventually be dispositioned. By enabling cost-effective decontamination, the technology could accelerate facility remediation programs and reduce pressure on limited TRU waste repository capacity.
