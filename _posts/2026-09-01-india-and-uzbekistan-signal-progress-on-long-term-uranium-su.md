India and Uzbekistan indicated that a long-term uranium supply deal is moving forward, as their leaders announced plans to grow bilateral trade.

India's Ministry of External Affairs said Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President Shavkat Mirziyoyev held wide-ranging talks in Tashkent during a state visit. According to the ministry, Modi appreciated the progress made on a long-term arrangement for supplying Uzbek uranium to India. He also reaffirmed the importance of cooperating on critical minerals.

Bilateral trade passed USD1 billion for the first time last year. The leaders set a goal of USD5 billion in annual turnover by 2030.

The statements did not give volumes, prices or a signing date for the uranium arrangement. World Nuclear News said a supply deal looked likely to form part of the wider trade push.
