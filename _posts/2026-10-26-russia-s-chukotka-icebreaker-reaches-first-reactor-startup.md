Russia's new Chukotka nuclear-powered icebreaker has reached minimum controlled power on its starboard reactor unit, marking a key milestone in the vessel's commissioning. The ship is part of Russia's Project 22220 fleet and features two RITM-200 reactors, each producing 175 MW of thermal power with a 40-year service life and refuelling every seven years.

The reactors are designed to operate in harsh Arctic conditions and can withstand significant vessel roll. Each unit weighs over 147 tonnes and measures 7.3 metres tall with a diameter of 3.3 metres. Following startup of both reactor units, the vessel will undergo comprehensive testing of its nuclear power plant systems, including controls, protection and monitoring systems.

The Chukotka represents continued Russian investment in nuclear-powered icebreaker technology to support operations in polar waters, where the reactors' sustained power output and long refuelling intervals reduce logistical demands compared to conventional propulsion.
