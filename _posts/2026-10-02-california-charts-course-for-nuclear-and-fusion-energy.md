California Governor Gavin Newsom signed two bills that will shape the state's long-term nuclear energy strategy. The first directs state agencies to conduct a formal assessment of whether new nuclear fission plants and advanced reactor technologies could support California's clean-energy goals and critical infrastructure resilience. The second establishes a fusion energy strategic planning process and mandates development of a regulatory framework for commercial fusion deployment.

The dual legislation reflects growing recognition that decarbonisation of an electricity-intensive economy may require diverse technologies. California, despite its historical ambivalence toward nuclear power, has experienced the grid stress created by variable renewables and periodic shortfalls during peak demand periods. Nuclear fission offers 24/7 baseload capacity, while fusion energy—though commercially nascent—could provide similar attributes if development timelines accelerate.

The bills position California as a policy leader in exploring advanced nuclear options alongside continued renewable expansion. The formal assessment and regulatory planning signal that state policymakers are moving beyond ideological resistance toward evidence-based evaluation of all available low-carbon technologies. Success in both areas could influence other jurisdictions considering advanced reactors or fusion ventures.
