Slovakia's Mochovce unit 4 began generating power at 15:33 on Monday, which operator Slovenské elektrárne described as the last major stage before the unit enters operation.

The reactor reached criticality, the point at which a fission chain reaction becomes self-sustaining, and minimum controlled power at the start of August. The Slovak Nuclear Regulatory Authority then inspected the plant and assessed documentation before approving the next commissioning step.

The change from the physical launch to the energy launch means reactor heat is now producing steam to turn the turbines. Output will rise in stages of 5%, 20%, 35%, 55%, 75% and 90%, with checks and tests at each level before the next increase.

Mochovce 4 is the latest unit to reach this point at the Slovak site. The staged power ascension is a standard part of bringing a new reactor into service. It lets operators and the regulator verify plant behaviour at progressively higher loads before the unit is handed over for commercial operation.
