The University of Wisconsin–Madison School of Medicine and Public Health has begun construction on a new 13,000-square-foot radioisotope production facility housing two cyclotrons within the Wisconsin Institutes for Medical Research complex. The $62 million project, funded by the National Institutes of Health, the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation, and private donations, is expected to complete by May 2028 and will increase the university's radioisotope production capacity by two- to eightfold depending on the isotope.

The facility will feature the first modern alpha-particle cyclotron installed in the United States, enabling production of alpha-emitting radioisotopes used in targeted radiotherapy and radiopharmaceutical research. Alpha emitters are increasingly studied for precision medicine applications in oncology and other therapeutic domains, particularly as theranostic development accelerates across the pharmaceutical and medical device sectors.

Expanded domestic production capacity addresses supply constraints that have historically limited research and clinical availability of specialized medical isotopes. The UW-Madison project reflects broader investment in U.S. cyclotron infrastructure to support medical isotope supply security and advance nuclear medicine research capabilities.
