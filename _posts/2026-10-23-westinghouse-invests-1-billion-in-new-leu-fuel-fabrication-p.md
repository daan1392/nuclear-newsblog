Westinghouse Electric committed $1 billion to construct a new fuel fabrication facility at its Columbia Fuel Fabrication site in Hopkins, South Carolina. The plant will produce LEU+ (low-enriched uranium plus) fuel—uranium enriched to levels above 5% U-235—enabling higher power density and extended operating cycles in commercial reactors. Production is targeted to begin in 2030 at a capacity of 600 metric tons of uranium per year.

The investment marks the first new fleet-scale nuclear fuel factory to be built in the United States in more than five decades. LEU+ fuel has already seen initial deployment: lead test assemblies containing Westinghouse's EnCore accident-tolerant fuel with LEU+ composition were loaded into a U.S. commercial reactor in 2025. Higher enrichment reduces the frequency of refueling and can improve economic performance, making the technology attractive to utilities managing aging fleets.

The project reflects both domestic fuel supply chain resilience and the broader industry trend toward advanced fuels. Westinghouse has already engaged with the Nuclear Regulatory Commission on the facility license amendment application. The timing aligns with anticipated near-term demand from existing reactor restarts and new builds expected to enter service in the 2030s.
