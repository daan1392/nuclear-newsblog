Slovakia's Mochovce 4, a 471 MWe VVER-440 reactor, was connected to the grid and began supplying its first electricity at approximately 20:41 on 15 October 2026, with the unit initially operating at 20% power output. The new unit's generation represents about 13% of Slovakia's total electricity consumption, significantly strengthening the country's electricity supply and decarbonisation objectives.

Mochovce 4 is the final unit of the four-reactor VVER station located in western Slovakia. The unit's completion marks the end of a multi-decade construction project that faced significant technical, financial and political challenges—a common experience for large nuclear new-build projects in Central Europe. Slovenske elektrárne, the plant operator, characterised the moment as realising the vision of generations of engineers and workers.

The reactor's grid connection is strategically important for Slovakia and the broader Central European region. The addition of 471 MWe of reliable, carbon-free generation provides baseload power stability during the European transition away from coal and fossil gas. The project's eventual completion demonstrates both the technical feasibility and the protracted timescales required for large reactor construction in modern developed economies.
