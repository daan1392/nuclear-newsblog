An International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) team concluded that Finland has a sound framework for nuclear and radiological emergencies, while recommending stronger national coordination.

The 10-day Emergency Preparedness Review ended on Wednesday. The team highlighted a national mechanism that brings together public authorities, industry and non-governmental organisations to keep critical societal functions running in an emergency. It also praised the training plan at Satasairaala hospital for treating people affected by radioactive contamination.

Among the areas for improvement, the team recommended strengthening coordination at the preparedness stage by establishing a National Coordinating Mechanism. It also called for better coordination during the response phase.

IAEA review missions are voluntary. They give countries an outside assessment against international safety standards and a set of recommendations to act on.
