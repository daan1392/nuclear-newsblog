Argentina's RA-10 multipurpose research reactor moved closer to operation as the country's atomic energy authorities reported pre-commissioning progress and took delivery of reactor-grade heavy water.

Twenty drums holding six tonnes of heavy water went from the Heavy Water Industrial Plant in Neuquén to the Ezeiza Atomic Centre. It has an isotopic purity of 99.86% and will fill the reactor's neutron reflector system. The reflector tank is a cylinder of Zircaloy-4 (a zirconium alloy) two metres in diameter and one metre high. In RA-10, heavy water moderates and reflects neutrons produced in the core.

At a presentation at the site, the National Atomic Energy Commission (CNEA) and the Secretariat for Nuclear Affairs said 530 people were finishing work and testing systems ahead of commissioning. Commissioning is due to start before the end of the year, and they spoke of a 2027 launch. They also outlined plans for private financing of the associated production plant.

The 30-megawatt open-pool reactor is meant to make products and services for health, science, technology and industry. It is expected to produce about 20% of global demand for molybdenum-99, the parent of technetium-99m, the most widely used radioisotope in diagnostic nuclear medicine.
