Ghana's Foreign Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa announced that Ghana has reached "concrete commitments" with Russia, including plans to develop a 2,000-megawatt nuclear power plant. The announcement positions Ghana to become the first African nation to deploy commercial nuclear power generation, though the project remains in early stages with no existing operational nuclear plants on the continent.

According to reports, construction is targeted to begin by 2027, though the announcement provided limited technical details about reactor type, financing, or implementation timeline. Ghana previously explored nuclear power in the 1960s under President Kwame Nkrumah, but those efforts were discontinued following a military coup.

The initiative reflects broader African interest in nuclear energy as a carbon-free baseload source to support growing electricity demand and industrial development. Russia has been actively marketing its reactor technology globally as part of its energy diplomacy strategy.
