The Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation has issued a fusion-specific byproduct material licence to Type One Energy, preparing the way for the company's commercial fusion project.

Tennessee is the first US state to issue a licence under a regulatory framework designed for fusion machines. In 2023 the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) said it would regulate fusion energy systems using its existing approach for byproduct materials. Under that approach, fusion devices are not treated as nuclear reactors. Fusion combines hydrogen atoms into helium rather than splitting uranium atoms, and it does not involve special nuclear material such as plutonium, uranium-233 or enriched uranium.

The licence shows the approach working in practice at state level. US states that have agreements with the NRC can license byproduct materials themselves. This gives fusion developers a clear regulatory route that is separate from the reactor licensing process.
