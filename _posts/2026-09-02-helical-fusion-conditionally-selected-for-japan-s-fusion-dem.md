Tokyo-based Helical Fusion said Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry had conditionally selected it for a project to demonstrate fusion power generation in the 2030s.

The company was founded in October 2021 and builds on research by the National Institute for Fusion Science on the helical stellarator. A stellarator is a magnetic confinement design that uses twisted coils to hold plasma. Helical Fusion aims to achieve what it calls the world's first practical fusion power generation through its Helix Programme.

The programme plans to complete separate demonstrations of two major elements by the mid-2020s: the high-temperature superconducting magnet, and the blanket and divertor. The blanket absorbs energy from the plasma, and the divertor handles exhaust heat and particles. In the 2030s it plans integrated demonstrations using its final device, Helix HARUKA.

The selection is conditional, so further steps are still needed before the project is confirmed.
