An unexpected surge of nonnative eelgrass in the Tennessee River clogged the cooling water intake at Browns Ferry nuclear power plant in Athens, Alabama, requiring the shutdown of two reactors and derating of the third unit to approximately 78% power. Tennessee Valley Authority reported the incident cost approximately $15 million to $20 million in forgone revenue.

The International Atomic Energy Agency documented the same event as resulting in roughly 0.5 terawatt-hours of production loss, underscoring the significant impact of environmental disruptions on grid reliability and facility economics. The incident highlights vulnerabilities in cooling water supplies from inland water bodies, particularly during unusual biological events.

Plant operators identified the event as an opportunity to improve maintenance efficiency and resilience against similar environmental stressors.
