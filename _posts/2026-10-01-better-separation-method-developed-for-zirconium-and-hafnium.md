Researchers at Oregon State University have developed an improved method for separating zirconium and hafnium, two chemically similar elements crucial to the nuclear industry. The work, published in the Journal of the American Chemical Society, addresses a long-standing challenge in nuclear fuel and materials processing.

More than 90 percent of zirconium and hafnium derived from zirconium silicate deposits enters the nuclear sector. High-purity zirconium is widely used for nuclear fuel rod cladding—the protective tubes that contain reactor fuel—while high-purity hafnium is employed in specialized control rods, including those used in nuclear submarines. The similarity of these elements has traditionally made separation energy-intensive and costly.

The new separation approach uses different crystal shapes in precipitates to achieve cleaner, more efficient separation. Improving this process could reduce costs and increase availability of these essential nuclear materials for power generation and naval propulsion applications.
