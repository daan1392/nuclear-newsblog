The University of Wisconsin–Madison School of Medicine and Public Health broke ground on a 13,000-square-foot facility to house two new cyclotrons for radioisotope production within its Wisconsin Institutes for Medical Research complex. The $62 million project, funded by the National Institutes of Health, the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation, and private donors, is expected to be completed by May 2028 and will increase the university's radioisotope production capacity by two- to eightfold depending on the isotope. The facility will host what the university described as the first modern alpha-particle cyclotron of its kind installed in the United States. Cyclotrons produce short-lived medical isotopes used in diagnostic imaging and therapeutic applications, and expanding domestic production capacity addresses supply challenges for nuclear medicine.
